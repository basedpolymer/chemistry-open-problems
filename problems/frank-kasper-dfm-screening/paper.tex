\begin{paperabstract}
The diblock foam model balances interface area against the second moments of
space-filling cells. An existing numerical screen of 121 catalogue
Frank--Kasper topologies, with three additional comparators, finds its lowest
value for the $\sigma$ phase. We audit that screen and provide exact,
reproducible geometric results. Integer certificates verify embedded
tetrahedral realizations of all 121 catalogue entries. A second implementation
checks six explicit foam partitions, their topology and their energy bounds,
including rigorous transport to the intended crystallographic boxes. Five
competitors have energies below the values printed in the source preprint;
for a relaxed $\sigma$ box we certify $\inf\DF\leq1.068575909$.
We strengthen the spherical relaxation to the uniform lower bound
$\DF>1.00000025$, using the Kepler sphere-packing theorem and exact rational
constants; a self-contained elementary version gives $\DF>1.000000004$.
We also certify global bounds for all affine images of the six stored meshes
by reducing their deformation problem to convex minimization.
Linear divergence calibrations give topology-wide lower bounds, certified
for all 121 entries. A separate theorem puts a ceiling below $1.066$ on
this particular lower-bound construction, so it cannot separate competitors
from the present $\sigma$ witness.
Eight additional exact witnesses improve the previously unrelaxed entries;
one also disproves the necessity of the centroid-cone admissibility test.
Neither these certificates nor
additional symmetry-breaking restarts establish the ordering of the topology
infima. We give complete proofs of the certified statements and identify the
missing global inequality. This is a partial result, not a solution of the
global minimization problem.
\end{paperabstract}

\section{The precise question and its scope}

Reddy et al.\autocite{Reddy2018} compare 11 Frank--Kasper (FK) structures and
body-centred cubic (BCC) partitions in the diblock foam model (DFM). Their
numerical comparison favours $\sigma$. Extending this comparison to the
catalogues of Dutour Sikirić, Delgado-Friedrichs and Deza\autocite{Dutour2010}
and Dutour Sikirić and Deza\autocite{Dutour2012} is the question studied here.
It is a question framed by this project; these sources do not formulate the
catalogue-wide DFM minimization as an explicit open conjecture.

The catalogues contain 84 and 37 structures, respectively. Their enumeration
is bounded by 20 and 21 cells in a reduced fundamental domain under a
fixed-point-free crystallographic group. This number is generally smaller
than the number of cells in the translation period used by the computations.
The catalogue is not a classification of all FK structures.

An admissible partition consists of bounded cells with positive volumes,
periodic under $L\mathbb Z^3$, with the prescribed face, edge and vertex
incidences. Faces may be curved: this is part of the source definitions.
Piecewise smooth and piecewise planar competitors are included. Cells have
spherical boundaries, faces are disks, and the topology is kept fixed.
Write $\mathcal P_T(L)$ for this class for topology $T$. With box relaxation,
the quantity to compare is
\[
 m_T=\inf_{\det L>0}\ \inf_{X\in\mathcal P_T(L)}\DF(X).
\]
Each topology uses the translation period represented by its input complex;
arbitrary larger supercells are a separate enlargement of this problem.
The desired conclusion would be $m_\sigma\leq m_T$ for every competitor $T$.
An optimizer returning a feasible $X_T$ establishes only
$m_T\leq\DF(X_T)$.

\section{Energy identity and a universal lower bound}

Let $V=\det L$, let $n$ be the number of cells per period, and set
$V_0=V/n=4\pi R_0^3/3$. For a lifted cell $\Omega_i$, define
\[
 v_i=|\Omega_i|,\qquad
 c_i=v_i^{-1}\int_{\Omega_i}x\,dx,\qquad
 J_i=\int_{\Omega_i}|x-c_i|^2\,dx.
\]
Let $A$ denote the total interface area per period, counting each interface
once, and let $J=\sum_i J_i$. Thus $\sum_i|\partial\Omega_i|=2A$ and
\[
 \Ar=\frac{2A/n}{4\pi R_0^2},\qquad
 \Mo=\frac{J/n}{4\pi R_0^5/5},\qquad
 \DF=(\Ar^2\Mo)^{1/3}.
\]
Substitution eliminates both $n$ and $\pi$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \boxed{\DF^3=\frac{20A^2J}{27V^3}.}
\end{equation}
The expression is unchanged by translation, rotation or uniform rescaling.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lower}
Every admissible partition satisfies $\DF\geq1$. In fact $\DF>1$ for a
face-to-face periodic tiling by bounded cells with positive interface areas.
This initial argument alone does not assert a uniform positive gap
between an infimum and 1; the next section establishes such a gap.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Euclidean isoperimetric inequality gives
\[
 A\geq\tfrac12(36\pi)^{1/3}\sum_i v_i^{2/3}.
\]
For completeness, the moment bound needs no convexity assumption. Fix the
centroid $c_i$ and let $B_i$ be the ball of volume $v_i$ centred there, with
radius $r_i$. The sets $\Omega_i\setminus B_i$ and $B_i\setminus\Omega_i$
have equal measure. On the former $|x-c_i|^2\geq r_i^2$, and on the latter
$|x-c_i|^2\leq r_i^2$. Subtracting the two integrals proves
\[
 J_i\geq\int_{B_i}|x-c_i|^2\,dx
       =\frac35\left(\frac3{4\pi}\right)^{2/3}v_i^{5/3}.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:energy} consequently yields
\[
 \DF^3\geq
 \frac{\bigl(\sum_i v_i^{2/3}\bigr)^2\sum_i v_i^{5/3}}{V^3}\geq1.
\]
The last inequality is Hölder applied to
$v_i=(v_i^{2/3})^{2/3}(v_i^{5/3})^{1/3}$ with exponents $3/2$ and 3.
Equality with all $v_i>0$ requires equal volumes and spherical cells.
Two disjoint equal-volume balls cannot share a face of positive area, so
they cannot form the stipulated tiling. This excludes pointwise equality,
but is not a compactness argument for a uniform gap.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cellwise}
For an isolated curved cell, its FK face-incidence graph does not improve
the ball constants in Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}. This cellwise relaxation
cannot distinguish catalogue entries.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each FK cell graph is the graph of a polyhedral sphere. Map this sphere
homeomorphically onto a round sphere and regard its images of vertices,
edges and faces as a subdivision of that round sphere. The resulting curved
cell has precisely that face-incidence graph and achieves both ball
constants. If nonzero dihedral features are required, arbitrarily small
smooth or piecewise planar perturbations approach the same constants.
For any list of cell types, the relaxation that forgets compatibility
between neighbouring cells can therefore use equal-volume balls for all
types. It has value 1. Compatibility between different cells is exactly
what this relaxation has discarded.
\end{proof}

\section{A uniform gap above 1}\label{sec:gap}

The gap below is intentionally modest. It controls all periods and all
positive cell volumes, but is far too weak to order FK topology infima.
Its purpose is to retain a quantitative part of the compatibility between
cells that the isolated-ball relaxation loses.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:moment-defect}
Let $B$ be the ball of volume $v$ and radius $r$ centred at the centroid of
a cell $\Omega$, and put $t=|B\setminus\Omega|/v$. Then
\[
 J_\Omega-\frac35vr^2\ \geq\ \frac23vr^2t^2.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The missing and excess sets both have volume $tv$. The smallest extra
moment comes from adding the shell just outside $B$ and removing the
shell just inside its boundary. This follows directly by comparing
$|x-c|^2$ on each side of the corresponding shell boundary. Thus the
moment excess is at least
\[
 \frac35vr^2\bigl((1+t)^{5/3}+(1-t)^{5/3}-2\bigr).
\]
The expression in parentheses has value and derivative zero at zero.
Its second derivative is
$\frac{10}{9}((1+t)^{-1/3}+(1-t)^{-1/3})\geq20/9$ for $0\leq t<1$,
by convexity. Two integrations and continuity at $t=1$ give the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:packing-gap}
Let $\rho<1$ be an upper bound for the density of any periodic packing of
equal balls in $\mathbb R^3$. Choose $0<\eta,\lambda,\tau<1$, and set
\[
 a=1-\eta,\quad b=1+\eta,\quad
 q=\frac{a(1-\lambda)}b,\quad
 C=\min\left\{\eta^2,\frac{10}{9}a^2\tau^2\right\}.
\]
If $a^3\lambda^2(3-\lambda)/2>2\tau b^3$, every admissible partition obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packing-gap}
 \DF^3\geq 1+C\left(1-\frac{\rho}{q^3}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each cell let $r_i=(3v_i/(4\pi))^{1/3}$ and let $t_i$ be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:moment-defect}. Write
$\mathbb E f=V^{-1}\sum_i v_i f_i$. Uniformly rescale the partition so that
$\mathbb E(1/r)=1$. The area bound now gives $A\geq3V/2$, hence
\[
 \DF^3\geq\frac{5J}{3V}
 \geq\mathbb E r^2+\frac{10}{9}\mathbb E(r^2t^2).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\mathbb E r\geq1$. Consequently, for
$\delta=\DF^3-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-budget}
 \mathbb E\left((r-1)^2+\frac{10}{9}r^2t^2\right)\leq\delta.
\end{equation}
Call a cell good if $a\leq r_i\leq b$ and $t_i\leq\tau$.
Every other cell contributes at least $C$ per unit of its volume weight
to the left-hand side. The good cells therefore occupy a fraction at
least $1-\delta/C$ of the volume. This estimate remains valid when that
lower bound is negative.

Consider any two distinct good cells in the infinite periodic tiling,
including different translates of the same representative. Their
centroid balls $B_i,B_j$ have intersection volume at most
$t_iv_i+t_jv_j\leq2\tau(4\pi/3)b^3$: the cells themselves are disjoint
up to sets of measure zero. If their centres had distance at most
$2a(1-\lambda)$, the radius-$a$ balls at those centres would intersect in
volume at least
\[
 \frac{4\pi}{3}a^3\frac{\lambda^2(3-\lambda)}2.
\]
This is the volume of two spherical caps of height $a\lambda$.
The assumed strict inequality excludes such a pair. Thus radius
$R=a(1-\lambda)$ balls at all good centres form a periodic packing.

If there are $m$ good representatives, their total cell volume is at
most $m(4\pi/3)b^3$. The packing density is consequently at least
$(1-\delta/C)(R/b)^3=(1-\delta/C)q^3$ and at most $\rho$.
Rearrangement proves~\eqref{eq:packing-gap}. No lower bound on any
individual cell volume or on the box aspect ratio was assumed.
\end{proof}

For a self-contained packing estimate, the classical Blichfeldt weight
$f(x)=\max\{1-|x|^2/2,0\}$ gives $\rho\leq5/(4\sqrt2)$.
Indeed, for unit-ball centres separated by at least 2, let $m$ centres
lie at distance less than $\sqrt2$ from a point $x$. If $m>0$, then
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^m|c_i-x|^2
 =\frac1m\sum_{i<j}|c_i-c_j|^2+m|\bar c-x|^2\geq2(m-1).
\]
It follows that $\sum_i f(x-c_i)\leq1$; for $m=0$ this is immediate.
Integration over one period, with
$\int_{\mathbb R^3}f=16\pi\sqrt2/15$, proves the density bound.
The historical reference is Blichfeldt\autocite{Blichfeldt1929}; the
argument needed here has just been given in full.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:uniform-gap}
Every admissible partition satisfies $\DF>1.000000004$ by the elementary
argument above. Using the established Kepler theorem, the stronger bound
is $\DF>1.00000025$. Both constants are uniform in topology, number of
cells per period, positive cell volumes and nonsingular box shape.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the elementary version choose
$(\eta,\lambda,\tau)=(3/4000,1/32,7/10000)$ and
$u=1.000000004$. Set $p=q^3(1-(u^3-1)/C)$.
Exact rational arithmetic verifies the strict lens condition, $p>0$,
and $p^2>25/32=\rho^2$. Equation~\eqref{eq:packing-gap} then gives
$\DF>u$.

The Kepler theorem\autocite{Hales2017} gives
$\rho=\pi/\sqrt{18}$. Choose
$(\eta,\lambda,\tau)=(9/2500,7/100,7/2000)$ and $u=1.00000025$.
The same exact checks now give $p>0$ and $p^2>242/441>\pi^2/18$,
where the latter inequality uses $\pi<22/7$.
For example, that elementary bound follows by integrating the positive
function $x^4(1-x)^4/(1+x^2)$ on $(0,1)$: its integral is $22/7-\pi$.
The script \texttt{check\_uniform\_gap.py} verifies all rational constants
and writes their strict margins. It does not reverify the Kepler theorem
or formalize the geometric proof.
\end{proof}

Thus degenerating sequences cannot approach energy 1. This is not a
compactness theorem near $\sigma$: the A15 vanishing-cell example below
is compatible with the new bound, which is much smaller than $1.068575909$.

\section{Why the integer geometry certificates prove embeddedness}

The following elementary degree statement makes the geometric verification
precise. A periodic piecewise affine map is described by finitely many
vertex representatives and integer lattice shifts.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:degree}
Let $K$ be a closed connected oriented triangulated 3-manifold and let
$f:K\to\mathbb R^3/L\mathbb Z^3$ be piecewise affine. Suppose its determinant
on every oriented tetrahedron is strictly positive. If the sum of its
tetrahedron volumes is $\det L$, then $f$ is a homeomorphism.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Integrating the volume form gives $\deg(f)=1$, since
\[
 \deg(f)\det L=\sum_\tau\operatorname{vol}(f(\tau))=\det L.
\]
A regular value outside the finite image of the two-dimensional skeleton
has one preimage: all local orientation signs are positive.

To include the skeleton, the fibre over any point is finite, since $f$ is
injective on each tetrahedron. Isolate each preimage in a neighbourhood
whose boundary does not map to the chosen value. Its local degree is
positive: a sufficiently close regular value in the image of an incident
tetrahedron has at least one positively oriented preimage in that
neighbourhood, and every other contribution is nonnegative. The sum of
these positive local degrees is 1. Thus every fibre is a singleton.
The continuous bijection from a compact space to the torus is a
homeomorphism.
\end{proof}

\subsection{All 121 catalogue topologies}

The certificate stores fractional vertex positions on the grid $2^{-44}$.
It lists the periodic tetrahedra of each catalogue contact complex.
Crystallographic expansion and floating-point coordinates are used only
to propose these data. The verifier uses integers for the following tests:
every tetrahedron has nonzero volume; after positive orientation every
triangle occurs twice with cancelling orientations; the vertex links are
connected oriented triangulated 2-spheres; and the sum of determinants is
exactly $6\,2^{132}$, the value for a unit-volume torus.

The face pairing and spherical links give a 3-manifold. The degree argument
then verifies its embedded periodic realization, not merely a volume
identity within a tolerance. Connectedness is also forced by positivity
and total degree one: every closed component would have positive integer
degree.

In each vertex link the verifier checks 12, 14, 15 or 16 vertices, all of
degree 5 or 6, exactly twelve of degree 5, and no edge joining two
degree-6 vertices. The dual cells therefore have the four FK types, with
isolated hexagons. The barycentric dual of the certified triangulation is
a genuine embedded foam with that topology.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:catalogue}
The 121 integer data sets in
\texttt{certificates/catalogue\_topology.npz} give embedded periodic FK
complexes with the incidence structures extracted from the 84 part-I and
37 part-II catalogue files. All 121 exact checks pass.
\end{theorem}

This verifies the supplied realizations. The identification of the
catalogue and its enumeration range comes from the source papers and
archive\autocite{Dutour2010,Dutour2012,CatalogueData}, not from a new
enumeration in this work. The manifest retains hashes of the CGD inputs.
An entry that is not Delaunay at its supplied coordinates is not thereby
proved incapable of another Voronoi realization.

\subsection{The six energy witnesses}

Each energy certificate instead stores the actual refined foam triangles,
their shared vertex representatives, periodic shifts, cell incidences and
a chosen interior apex for each cell. The audit checks every cell boundary
as a connected oriented 2-sphere, including circular vertex links. Each
coarse interface is a disk with one boundary circle. Periodic cell
interfaces are unique, each foam edge meets three cells and faces, and
each foam vertex meets four cells and six faces. The extracted labelled
periodic dual tetrahedra agree exactly with the corresponding complex
reconstructed from the crystallographic registry.

Coning the cell boundaries to their apices gives the requisite triangulated
3-manifold. Strict positivity of all cone determinants and the exact
volume identity then allow Lemma~\ref{lem:degree} to be applied. Thus the
triangles describe an embedded partition with the declared topology.

\section{Exact integration and box correction}

For an oriented boundary triangle with vertices $a,b,c$ relative to its
cell apex, let $d=\det(a,b,c)>0$ and $w=d/6$. The contribution of its cone
to volume, first moment and second moment is
\begin{align}
 v_\tau&=w,\qquad
 M_\tau=\frac w4(a+b+c),\label{eq:first}\\
 S_\tau&=\frac w{20}\left(|a+b+c|^2+|a|^2+|b|^2+|c|^2\right).
 \label{eq:second}
\end{align}
Indeed, for the three nonzero barycentric coordinates of a uniform
tetrahedron, the averages are $1/4$, their squares have average $1/10$,
and distinct products have average $1/20$. Expanding the integrands proves
these formulas. After summing over a cell,
\[
 J_i=S_i-\frac{|M_i|^2}{v_i}.
\]
All these quantities are rational on the integer grid. A triangle area
equals $\sqrt{q}/2$, where $q$ is the squared integer cross-product norm,
with the grid scale understood. Integer floor and ceiling square roots
enclose every area. Substitution into~\eqref{eq:energy}, followed by exact
rational comparisons of cubes, encloses the energy of the particular mesh.
The lower endpoint is \emph{not} a lower bound on $m_T$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:transport}
Let $T$ be linear with $\|T-I\|_2\leq\varepsilon<1$ and $\det T>0$.
For any partition $X$,
\[
 \DF(TX)\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^2}{(1-\varepsilon)^2}\DF(X).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Areas increase by at most $(1+\varepsilon)^2$. Centroids transform by $T$,
so $J(TX)\leq\det(T)(1+\varepsilon)^2J(X)$, while
$V(TX)=\det(T)V(X)$. Consequently~\eqref{eq:energy} gives
$\DF(TX)\leq(1+\varepsilon)^2\det(T)^{-2/3}\DF(X)$.
Since $\det T\geq(1-\varepsilon)^3$, the claimed bound follows.
\end{proof}

For each fixed box the audit forms $T=B\widehat L^{-1}$, where
$\widehat L$ is the stored dyadic box and $B$ is the exact intended box.
Arb ball arithmetic at 192-bit precision proves
$\|T-I\|_F<10^{-12}$. Rectangular aspect ratios are exact rationals;
the hexagonal box uses $\sqrt3/2$. The Frobenius bound implies the operator
norm bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:transport}. The correction leaves all
nine-decimal upper bounds unchanged.

\section{Global affine minimization of a fixed partition}\label{sec:affine}

One part of the optimization does admit a convex reduction. Here the
entire cell geometry is fixed before one applies a common affine map.
This does not include independent changes of faces or cell volumes.

Let $S=\sum_i\int_{\Omega_i}(x-c_i)(x-c_i)^{\mathsf T}dx$ be the
centred moment tensor of a fixed partition $X$, with period volume $V$.
It is positive definite. Integrate once over each interface, and write
$n$ for its unit normal; its sign is immaterial. For $G\succ0$ define
\[
 a(G)=\int\sqrt{n^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}n}\,dA,
 \qquad j(G)=\operatorname{tr}(GS).
\]
If $T^{\mathsf T}T=G$, area transforms by
$|\det T|\,|T^{-\mathsf T}n|$, the moment tensor by
$|\det T|TST^{\mathsf T}$, and volume by $|\det T|$.
All determinant factors cancel from the energy identity:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-energy}
 \DF(TX)^3=\frac{20}{27V^3}a(G)^2j(G).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:affine}
The minimization of $\DF(TX)$ over nonsingular affine maps is equivalent
to minimizing the convex function $a(G)$ subject to
$G\succ0$ and $\operatorname{tr}(GS)=j_0$, for any fixed $j_0>0$.
At a chosen $G\succ0$ put $a=a(G)$, $j=j(G)$ and
\[
 K=\int\frac{G^{-1}nn^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}}
                   {\sqrt{n^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}n}}\,dA.
\]
If $\kappa S-K$ is positive semidefinite and $b=(3a-\kappa j)/2>0$,
then every nonsingular affine map satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-lower}
 \DF(TX)^3\geq\frac{20b^2j}{27V^3}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $n$, the identity
\[
 \sqrt{n^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}n}
 =\sup_{z\ne0,\ n^{\mathsf T}z\geq0}
       \frac{n^{\mathsf T}z}{\sqrt{z^{\mathsf T}Gz}}
\]
is Cauchy--Schwarz in the $G$ metric, with equality at $z=G^{-1}n$.
Each function in the supremum is convex in $G$, since $u\mapsto u^{-1/2}$
is convex for $u>0$. Integration proves convexity of $a$.
Scaling $G$ multiplies $a$ by the inverse square root and $j$ by the
scale, leaving $a^2j$ unchanged. This proves the normalization claim.

Differentiation gives $\nabla a(G)=-K/2$, and
$\operatorname{tr}(KG)=a$. For any $H\succ0$ normalized by
$\operatorname{tr}(HS)=j$, the supporting-plane inequality gives
\[
 a(H)\geq a-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(K(H-G))
 =\tfrac32a-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(KH)
 \geq\tfrac12(3a-\kappa j)=b.
\]
The last step follows from $\kappa S-K\succeq0$ and $H\succ0$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:affine-energy} completes the global bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Topology-wide divergence bounds and their limitation}

Unlike the affine families above, the next bound allows arbitrary internal
cell deformations. Label a contact edge by $(i,j,s)$ if cell $i$ in the
base period meets cell $j$ translated by $Ls$, with $s\in\mathbb Z^3$.
Let $W$ be a real $3\times3$ matrix of positive determinant and let
$z_1,\ldots,z_n\in\mathbb R^3$. Suppose that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fieldnorm}
 |z_i-z_j+Ws|\leq1\qquad\text{for every contact edge }(i,j,s).
\end{equation}
These conditions use only the periodic topology.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:topocalibration}
For every admissible partition with these incidences, arbitrary positive
cell volumes and any box $L$ of positive determinant,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:topocalibration}
 A\geq3(\det W)^{1/3}V^{2/3},\qquad
 \DF\geq\left(\frac{6\det W}{\pi n}\right)^{2/9}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $B=WL^{-1}$ and, on the lifted cell indexed by $(i,k)$, use the field
$\xi_{i,k}(x)=B(x-Lk)+z_i$. Its divergence is $\operatorname{tr}B$.
On a shared interface the difference of the two fields is
$z_i-z_j+Ws$, independent of position. The divergence theorem and
\eqref{eq:fieldnorm} therefore give $V\operatorname{tr}B\leq A$.
This argument applies to curved as well as planar faces.

Replacing $(W,z_i)$ by $(RW,Rz_i)$ for any proper rotation $R$ preserves
\eqref{eq:fieldnorm}. The maximum of $\operatorname{tr}(RWL^{-1})$
over these rotations is the sum of the three singular values of $WL^{-1}$:
its determinant is positive, so the maximizing orthogonal factor can be
chosen proper. Their arithmetic--geometric mean is at least
$3(\det W/V)^{1/3}$, proving the area bound.

Ball rearrangement and Jensen's inequality give, without requiring equal
cell volumes,
\[
 J\geq\frac35\left(\frac3{4\pi}\right)^{2/3}
       \sum_i v_i^{5/3}
 \geq\frac35\left(\frac3{4\pi}\right)^{2/3}V^{5/3}n^{-2/3}.
\]
Substitute this and the area bound into \eqref{eq:energy}. Cubing the
resulting inequality for $\DF^3$ yields
$\DF^9\geq36(\det W)^2/(\pi^2 n^2)$, as claimed.
\end{proof}

Proposals maximize $\log\det W$ under \eqref{eq:fieldnorm}, with
$W$ symmetric positive definite and $z_1=0$. This is a convex optimization
problem; symmetry of $W$ loses no positive-determinant candidates, by a
common left rotation (polar decomposition). Solver status is not a proof.
For each saved proposal we round $W,z_i$ to integer arrays and divide them
by one integer larger than the square root of the maximum squared edge
norm. The verifier checks every edge inequality with integers, checks
$\det W>0$, and uses 192-bit outward arithmetic for $\pi$ and the final
decimal bound. It also rechecks the exact catalogue complex.
Thus verification requires no optimizer, external source archive or
assumption of numerical optimality.

All 121 certificates pass; 34 improve the uniform bound $1.00000025$.
Selected certified topology-wide lower bounds are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 m_{\mathrm{A15}}&\geq1.008666655,&\qquad m_H&\geq1.008808885,\\
 m_\sigma&\geq1.004288649,& m_{\mathrm{E11}}&\geq1.004321474.
 \end{aligned}
\]

There is a structural obstruction to obtaining the desired separating
bound by improving this optimization alone.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:calibrationceiling}
If the periodic contact triangulation has $t$ tetrahedra and $n$ vertices,
every feasible pair $(W,z)$ in \eqref{eq:fieldnorm} satisfies
\[
 \det W\leq\frac{t}{6\sqrt2},\qquad
 \left(\frac{6\det W}{\pi n}\right)^{2/9}
 \leq\left(\frac{t^2}{2\pi^2 n^2}\right)^{1/9}.
\]
For all 121 catalogue entries the rightmost number is less than $1.066$.
This is a ceiling on the bound in \eqref{eq:topocalibration},
\emph{not an upper bound on any physical energy or topology infimum}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Map the contact vertex $(i,k)$ to $-z_i+Wk$, and extend affinely over
each contact tetrahedron. Every mapped edge has length at most one by
\eqref{eq:fieldnorm}. The map is equivariant from the original torus to
the torus with period matrix $W$. Its induced lattice map is the identity
in these coordinates; it is homotopic to the corresponding linear map,
by straight interpolation of its periodic displacement. Hence it has
degree one. Equivalently, cancellation of the oriented periodic faces
gives the signed-volume identity
\[
 \sum_{\text{oriented contact tetrahedra}}
      \operatorname{Vol}_{\mathrm{signed}}(\text{image})=\det W.
\]
The map may fold or collapse some tetrahedra; neither possibility affects
this identity. For each saved certificate the verifier additionally
checks the identity by exact integer determinants.

For completeness, a tetrahedron with all six edges of length at most one
has volume at most $1/(6\sqrt2)$. Indeed, for its vertices $q_0,\ldots,q_3$
and their mean $\bar q$, put
$S=\sum_a(q_a-\bar q)(q_a-\bar q)^{\mathsf T}$. Then
\[
 \operatorname{tr}S=\tfrac14\sum_{a<b}|q_a-q_b|^2\leq\tfrac32,
 \qquad \operatorname{Vol}^2=\frac{\det S}{9}
 \leq\frac{(\operatorname{tr}S/3)^3}{9}\leq\frac1{72}.
\]
Taking absolute values in the signed-volume sum proves the determinant
bound. Direct counts in the independently rechecked catalogue give
$t/n\leq124/21$, with equality for part-II Y04--Y06. Therefore the
method's ceiling is at most
\[
 \left(\frac{(124/21)^2}{2\pi^2}\right)^{1/9}
 =1.0652530398\ldots<1.066.
\]
The final strict inequality is checked with outward arithmetic.
\end{proof}

These calibrations supply valid topology-dependent information over the
full admissible classes, but cannot reach the present witness level
$1.068575909$. A stronger method must use information discarded by this
separate area and ball-moment estimate, or a different lower certificate.

\subsection{A valid cell need not be star-shaped about its centroid}

A new continuation uses a moving auxiliary apex for each cell. For the
oriented determinant $d$ of each boundary cone, a logarithmic barrier
$-\log(d-d_0)$ enforces positivity, with $d_0$ chosen to correspond to
$10^{-7}$ times the mean cell volume. The energy always uses the true
volume centroid; the auxiliary apex serves only to certify geometry.
Six barrier weights, $10^{-1},\ldots,10^{-6}$, are used at mesh level zero,
with up to 5000 L-BFGS iterations per weight and a freely strained box.
Some stages reach their iteration limit with non-negligible gradients.
They provide trial partitions, not stationary or optimal solutions.
This search uses cells star-shaped about some auxiliary point, which is
still only a sufficient subclass of the admissible curved cells.

The part-II Y04 example supplies a precise correction to the use of the
older centroid-cone diagnostic. Its saved dyadic partition has 42 cells,
the exact prescribed dual complex, positive auxiliary cone determinants
and an exact degree-one volume sum. Its energy is at most $1.254466833$.
Nevertheless, 184 boundary cones based at the \emph{true volume centroids}
have strictly negative determinants, verified using rational first moments.
Thus the partition is embedded although the centroid-cone test fails.
Positivity at the centroid is a sufficient condition under the other
manifold hypotheses, not a necessary condition for admissibility.
Failure of that diagnostic alone cannot justify discarding a topology or
calling its cells self-intersecting.

\section{The missing global step}

A certified ordering would follow from a feasible $\sigma$ energy $U$ and
bounds $L_T$ such that $\DF(X)\geq L_T>U$ for \emph{every} admissible
$X$ of every other topology. No such bounds are supplied by the screen.
Refinement and restarts do not supply them either. A positive Hessian at a
particular stationary mesh would only be a local statement, and would
still require a continuum error argument.

The obstruction is not missing optimization software. It is control over
all admissible curved faces, all positive cell volumes and all allowed
box shapes, including degenerating sequences. A fixed-topology class need
not be closed under such degenerations, so an attained minimizer cannot
be presumed.

\subsection{A certified restricted family and a finite degeneration}

The one-parameter flat-face A15 family in the source SI, Eq.~(S16)
(preprint Eq.~(A16)), has energy
\[
 F_c^3=\frac5{55296}(a+bc^2)^2(3c^4-5c^3+10),\qquad
 a=12+12\sqrt6,\quad b=6\sqrt5-4\sqrt6-3>0.
\]
Here the cubic period has side length 2, and the two $Z_{12}$ cells have
volume $c^3/2$ each. The other six cells have volume $(4-c^3/2)/3$.
The parameter range on which this formula describes the family includes
positive $c$ approaching zero and the sample $c=1.22$ used below.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:a15}
For every $c\geq0$ the displayed algebraic expression satisfies
$F_c>1.0699$. Moreover $F_{1.22}<1.0701$. Thus no admissible member of
this particular flat A15 family improves the certified $\sigma$ witness.
This is not a lower bound for unrestricted curved A15 cells.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let
$P(c)=5(a+bc^2)^2(3c^4-5c^3+10)-55296(1.0699)^3$.
On $[0,2]$ we verify positivity by Bernstein coefficients, using outward
ball arithmetic with 192-bit precision for the radicals. The six intervals
have endpoints
\[
 0,\quad 1,\quad 9/8,\quad 19/16,\quad 5/4,\quad 3/2,\quad 2.
\]
For clarity, the complete conversion rule is as follows. If
$P(c)=\sum_{j=0}^8p_jc^j$, then on $[l,l+h]$ set
$q_i=h^i\sum_{j=i}^8\binom ji p_jl^{j-i}$. Its Bernstein coefficients
are $\beta_k=\sum_{i=0}^k q_i\binom ki/\binom8i$.
The verifier proves every $\beta_k>0$ on each interval. Since the
Bernstein basis is nonnegative and sums to one, $P>0$ there.

For $c\geq2$, the derivative of $(a+bc^2)^2(3c^4-5c^3+10)$ equals
\[
 c(a+bc^2)\bigl[bc^3(24c-35)+3ac(4c-5)+40b\bigr]>0.
\]
The already checked value at 2 completes the half-line argument.
Direct outward evaluation at the rational sample $61/50$ proves the
upper bound. All steps are implemented in
\texttt{scripts/check\_a15\_family.py}; no sampled minimization is used.
\end{proof}

As $c\downarrow0$, the two $Z_{12}$ cells disappear, but
\[
 F_c\longrightarrow
 \left(\frac{50(12+12\sqrt6)^2}{55296}\right)^{1/3}
 =1.157131807984\ldots.
\]
Thus bounded energy alone does not prevent vanishing cell volumes even
within an FK family. This particular limit is above the $\sigma$ witness;
it does not settle other degenerating families or show that degenerations
can occur near the observed optimum. Any exclusion of those limits needs
an additional argument.

\subsection{Necessary stationarity is not global minimality}

For a smooth interface oriented outward from cell $i$ toward cell $j$,
let $h=\operatorname{div}_{\!S}n$ be the sum of principal curvatures. A
normal displacement $u$ has first variations
\[
 \delta A=\int h u\,dS,\qquad
 \delta J=\int\bigl(|x-c_i|^2-|x-c_j|^2\bigr)u\,dS.
\]
The dependence of the centroids causes no additional term, since
$\int_{\Omega_i}(x-c_i)\,dx=0$. Thus an interior stationary partition
in a fixed box must satisfy
\[
 2J h+A\bigl(|x-c_i|^2-|x-c_j|^2\bigr)=0
\]
on each smooth interface. Triple-line variations additionally give the
usual equal-tension force balance. These are necessary conditions, not a
convexity or uniqueness theorem.

\subsection{A sufficient form of a global lower certificate}

Normalize $V=1$ by scaling. For positive $A,J$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearization}
 \DF=\frac{20^{1/3}}9\inf_{s>0}\bigl(2sA+s^{-2}J\bigr),
\end{equation}
because the optimizing value satisfies $s^3=J/A$.
This infimum identity warns against bounding just one chosen linear
combination: a lower bound is needed uniformly in $s$.

Here is one possible sufficient mechanism. Index all periodic copies of
the cells by $\alpha$, with centres $c_\alpha$, and choose continuously
differentiable vector fields $\xi_\alpha$ equivariant under lattice
translation. Suppose that for every pair and every position
$|\xi_\alpha-\xi_\beta|\leq2s$. Summing the divergence theorem over
the cells in a period gives
\[
 2sA+s^{-2}J\geq
 \int_{\mathbb R^3/L\mathbb Z^3}
 \inf_\alpha\left(\operatorname{div}\xi_\alpha(x)
                  +s^{-2}|x-c_\alpha|^2\right)dx.
\]
At a shared interface this follows from
$(\xi_\alpha-\xi_\beta)\cdot n\leq2s$; the remaining inequality is
pointwise. A uniform lower bound $9U/20^{1/3}$ on the right for all $s$,
centres and allowed boxes would prove $\DF\geq U$ through
\eqref{eq:linearization}. No fields attaining such a bound near the
observed $\sigma$ value have been constructed here. Restrictions to a
fixed set of centres would leave the original question unresolved.

The standalone catalogue certificate needs no third-party archive at

The energy audit has a separate exact integration implementation from the
optimizer and the original certificate program. It also verifies manifold
links, interface topology and affine transport. Its regression checks
reject reversed triangles, missing tetrahedra, duplicated faces and a
pinched surface, and check invariance under translation and scaling.
These are implementation checks, not a formal proof-assistant verification.

The topology-calibration verifier additionally checks all periodic edge
norms and mapped signed-volume identities using integers. Four tests cover
an analytic cubic torus, gauge translations, false norm and energy claims,
and a missing tetrahedron. Three barrier tests check a directional derivative,
translation invariance, boundary rejection and the independence of the true
centroid calculation from the auxiliary apex.

The affine-orbit tests additionally check the exact tensor of a cube,
its transformation under a non-diagonal shear and translation, the known
affine minimum of the cube, and rejection of false dual certificates.

This continuation used a single agent, as requested. The new code and
mathematical arguments have not undergone a fresh independent review.
The global ordering $m_\sigma\leq m_T$ remains unproved; the numerical
screen, the exact feasibility statements and the global enclosure
\eqref{eq:global} are the conclusions supported by the present work.
