\begin{paperabstract}
Jäschke and Skogestad (Comput.\ Chem.\ Eng.\ 2014) showed that optimal operation of a heat-exchanger network with
stream splits only requires temperature measurements: with an arithmetic-mean temperature-difference (AMTD) model, the
marginal cost of a split branch is claimed to be a sum \(\sum_i p_{ij}a_{ij}\) of recursively defined ``Jäschke
temperatures''. They proved this for branches with \(M_j\le2\) exchangers, reported a proof of \(M_j=3\) for constant
hot-stream temperatures, and conjectured the general case. We show that the published recursion is wrong for every
\(M_j\ge3\). Hence the conjecture is false, and so is the \(M_j=3\) case reported as proved. The error in that proof is
located: the raw computer-algebra output printed in the paper is correct, but a manual simplification drops a term. An
exact rational counterexample has the paper's controlled variable equal to zero while the true gradient is
\(-4972391424/1518467647\). We prove the corrected recursion \(a_i=c_i(\theta_i+\theta_{i-1}-\sum_{l<i}a_l)\) for every
\(M_j\), with a half-page proof based on the affine structure of each exchanger. On the paper's own validation case, the
published controlled variable vanishes at a split that differs from the AMTD optimum by \(0.0026\), so the published
recursion alone produces a split error of the order the paper attributed to flatness and round-off.
\end{paperabstract}

\section{Source and exact statement}

J. Jäschke, S. Skogestad, \emph{Optimal operation of heat exchanger networks with stream split: Only temperature
measurements are required}, Computers and Chemical Engineering 70 (2014) 35--49,
doi:10.1016/j.compchemeng.2014.03.020~\autocite{JS14} (Manfred Morari special issue). Read in full: the publisher PDF
self-archived at
\url{https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/publications/2014/jaschke-hen-paper-cce-morari-issue/impress.pdf}, plus the older
preprint \url{.../old-version/HenCCE.pdf}. Page numbers below are journal page numbers.

\textbf{Model (Section 3.1, p.~37--38).} \(N\) parallel branches split from a cold feed (temperature \(T_0\),
heat-capacity flow rate \(w_0\)); branch \(j\) carries \(w_j = F_j c_{p0}\), \(\sum_j w_j = w_0\), and \(M_j\) heat
exchangers in series. For exchanger \(i\) on branch \(j\): cold outlet \(T_{i,j}\) (cold inlet \(T_{i-1,j}\),
\(T_{0,j} = T_0\)), hot inlet \(T_{hi,j}\), hot outlet \(T^{out}_{hi,j}\), hot heat-capacity flow rate \(w_{hi,j}\),
conductance \(UA_{i,j}\):
\[Q_{i,j} = w_j (T_{i,j} - T_{i-1,j}) \quad (9), \qquad Q_{i,j} = w_{hi,j}(T_{hi,j} - T^{out}_{hi,j}) \quad (10),\]
\[Q_{i,j} = UA_{i,j}\,\frac{(T_{hi,j} - T_{i,j}) + (T^{out}_{hi,j} - T_{i-1,j})}{2} \quad (12,\ \text{Assumption 3, AMTD}).\]
(The printed (9)--(10) contain the index misprints \(w_i\) for \(w_j\) and \(T^{out}_{i-1,j}\) for \(T^{out}_{hi,j}\);
the intended model is the one above, as Fig.~3 and (A.1)--(A.4) show.) Assumptions 1--2: single phase, constant heat
capacities. Cost (14): \(J = -\sum_j \sum_i p_{i,j} Q_{i,j}\). Shifted temperatures (15): \(\theta = T - T_0\).

\textbf{Theorem 2 (p.~39), verbatim.} ``\emph{Under Assumptions 1--3, and equal prices \(p_{i,j} = 1\) in the cost
function (14), the marginal costs for each branch \(j = 1 \dots N\) can be expressed as}
\[\frac{\partial J}{\partial w_j} = T_{J,j} \quad (16)\]
\emph{where \(T_{J,j}\) is the Jäschke Temperature on branch \(j\), defined as \(T_{J,j} = \sum_{i=1}^{M_j} a_{i,j}\)
(17), with the parameter \(a_{i,j}\) defined recursively as}
\[a_{i,j} = \frac{(\theta_{i,j} - \theta_{i-1,j})(\theta_{i,j} + \theta_{i-1,j} - a_{i-1,j})}{\theta_{hi,j} - \theta_{i-1,j}},
\qquad a_{0,j} = 0. \quad (18)\text{''}\]

\textbf{Theorem 3 (p.~39)} is the same with arbitrary prices: \(\partial J/\partial w_j = T^e_{J,j} = \sum_i p_{i,j}
a_{i,j}\) (19)--(20), with the same \(a_{i,j}\).

\textbf{The open statements, verbatim.}
\begin{itemize}
\item p.~39, after Theorem 2: ``\emph{In Appendix A we provide a proof of Theorem 2 for \(M_j = 1\) and \(M_j = 2\).
Moreover, we prove the case \(M_j = 3\) for heat exchangers with constant hot stream temperatures (this corresponds to
very large hot stream heat capacities). For \(M_j \ge 4\) we conjecture that the Theorem is true.}'' The same sentence
is repeated after Theorem 3.
\item p.~47, end of A.3: ``\emph{Although this expression was derived under the assumption of a constant hot stream
temperature \(T_{hi,j} = T^{out}_{hi,j}\), we conjecture that it is true for the case with a finite heat capacity
(non-constant hot streams).}''
\item p.~47, A.4 ``Conjecture for the general case (\(M \ge 4\))'': ``\emph{Unfortunately it was computationally not
feasible to perform the elimination for the case with \(M_j > 3\). From the recurring pattern, that coefficients of the
previous prices appear in a fixed structure in the coefficients of the next price, we conjecture the general formula
for the case with more than three heat exchangers on a line as}'' (A.14)--(A.16), identical to (18)--(20).
\item p.~45, Conclusions: ``\emph{For the case with 1--3 heat exchangers per line, we have proven {[}\ldots{]} that the
Jäschke Temperatures are equal to the marginal costs. The more general case with more than 3 heat exchangers is
conjectured.}''
\item p.~49, Appendix B: ``\emph{In conclusion, the numerical results for this case study strongly suggest that the
conjecture is true.}''
\end{itemize}

The recursion is unambiguous: (A.13) and (B.1) write it out for \(M_j = 3, 4\), and each new term subtracts only the
previous \(a_{i-1,j}\); the efficiency form (29) of Section 6.1, \(a_{i,j} = c_{i,j}(\theta_{i,j}+\theta_{i-1,j}-a_{i-1,j})\),
says the same. The conjecture concerns Theorems 2 \textbf{and} 3, and covers \(M_j \ge 4\) as well as \(M_j = 3\) with
finite hot heat capacities.

\section{Results}

Fix one branch and drop the index \(j\). Write \(w\) for its cold heat-capacity flow rate, \(\theta_0 = 0\), and
\(\Delta_i = \theta_i - \theta_{i-1}\). For each exchanger define
\[k_i = \frac{2}{UA_i} + \frac{1}{w_{h,i}} \ \ (1/\infty := 0), \qquad c_i = \frac{\theta_i - \theta_{i-1}}{\theta_{h,i} - \theta_{i-1}}\]
(the paper's ``efficiency'', eq.~27).

\textbf{Theorem A (corrected Jäschke temperature; all \(M \ge 1\)).} Under Assumptions 1--3, for every exchanger \(i\)
of the branch,
\[\frac{\partial Q_i}{\partial w} = \frac{a_i}{2}, \qquad
   a_i = c_i\Big(\theta_i + \theta_{i-1} - \sum_{l<i} a_l\Big),\]
where \(\partial/\partial w\) holds \(T_0\), the hot inlet temperatures, the \(w_{h,i}\) and the \(UA_i\) fixed. Hence
\[\frac{\partial J_j}{\partial w_j} = -\tfrac12\, T^{e*}_{J,j}, \qquad T^{e*}_{J,j} := \sum_{i=1}^{M_j} p_{i,j}\, a_{i,j},
   \qquad\text{and for } p_{i,j}\equiv 1:\ \frac{\partial J_j}{\partial w_j} = -\tfrac12\, T^*_{J,j},\]
where \(T^*_{J,j} = A_{M_j}\) is given by the affine recursion
\[A_0 = 0, \qquad A_i = (1 - c_i)\,A_{i-1} + c_i\,(\theta_i + \theta_{i-1}).\]
This is the same form as the cold-stream recursion \(\theta_i = (1-c_i)\theta_{i-1} + c_i\theta_{h,i}\). The formula
uses only the cold temperatures \(\theta_0,\dots,\theta_M\) and the hot inlet temperatures \(\theta_{h,i}\). Because the
factor \(-\tfrac12\) is common to all branches, equal marginal costs (Theorem 1 of the paper) is \textbf{exactly}
equivalent to \(T^{e*}_{J,1} = \dots = T^{e*}_{J,N}\). In particular the qualitative claim of the paper's title (under
AMTD, temperature measurements suffice to locate the optimal split) is true for every \(M_j\); what fails for
\(M_j \ge 3\) is the specific recursion (18) conjectured by the authors.

\textbf{Corollary B (the conjecture is false).} Let \(a^P_i\) denote the paper's recursion (18), and
\(d_i = a^P_i - a_i\). Then \(d_1 = d_2 = 0\) and \(d_i = c_i\,(A_{i-2} - d_{i-1})\) for \(i \ge 3\). In particular
\(d_3 = c_3\,a_1 = c_3\,\theta_1^2/\theta_{h,1}\), which is nonzero whenever exchangers 1 and 3 both transfer heat.
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The paper's formula is correct for \(M_j \le 2\) up to the harmless common factor \(-\tfrac12\)
(Section~\ref{sec:half}). So Examples 1--2 and Case studies 1--3 of the paper are unaffected.
\item \textbf{Theorem 3 (prices) is false for every \(M_j \ge 3\), even up to any normalisation constant.} Suppose
\(\sum_i p_i a^P_i = \lambda \sum_i p_i a_i\) for all prices. Then \(a^P_i = \lambda a_i\) for every \(i\). The case
\(i = 1\) (\(a^P_1 = a_1 \ne 0\)) forces \(\lambda = 1\), but \(a^P_3 = a_3 + c_3a_1 \ne a_3\).
\item \textbf{Theorem 2 (\(p \equiv 1\)) is false for every \(M_j \ge 3\).} The mechanism is general. Take any branch~1
with \(T^P_{J,1} := \sum_i a^P_i \ne T^*_{J,1}\) and \(T^P_{J,1} > 0\), and a branch~2 with a single exchanger. For one
exchanger \(\theta_{1,2} = c\,\theta_{h1,2}\) with \(c = 2/(1+w_2k_{1,2})\) independent of \(\theta_{h1,2}\), so
\(T_{J,2} = c^2\theta_{h1,2}\); choosing \(\theta_{h1,2} = T^P_{J,1}/c^2\) makes the paper's controlled variable
\(T^P_{J,1} - T_{J,2}\) exactly zero, while by Theorem A the true gradient is
\(\mathrm{d}J/\mathrm{d}w_1|_{w_2=w_0-w_1} = -\tfrac12(T^*_{J,1} - T^P_{J,1}) \ne 0\). This is independent of any
normalisation.
\begin{itemize}
\item \(M_1 = 3\): Section~\ref{sec:cex} gives an exact rational, physically meaningful instance.
\item \(M_1 \ge 4\): append to branch 1 of that instance \(M-3\) exchangers with conductance \(\varepsilon\) and hot
inlet temperatures different from \(\theta_3\). As \(\varepsilon \to 0^+\),
\(c_i = 2/(1 + w(2/\varepsilon + 1/w_{h,i})) \to 0\) for \(i \ge 4\), so \(d_i \to 0\) for \(i \ge 4\) and
\(T^P_{J,1} - T^*_{J,1} = \sum_i d_i \to c_3a_1 \ne 0\). For small \(\varepsilon > 0\), re-tune \(\theta_{h1,2}\) as above
so that the paper's controlled variable is again exactly zero; the gradient is then nonzero.
\item Direct instances: the verifier's script \texttt{verification/v2\_exact.py} builds exact physical counterexamples
with \(M_1 = 4\) and \(M_1 = 5\) (claims X3a, X3b), and the paper's own Appendix~B data give an \(M_1 = 4\)
counterexample to Theorem 3 (Section~\ref{sec:appB}, claim B3) and, with all prices set to 1, to Theorem 2.
\end{itemize}
The computer-algebra checks for \(M = 3,\dots,8\) and the random tests for \(M \le 8\) find the difference
\(T^P_J - T^*_J\) to be a nonzero rational function, and nonzero in every draw.
\item The case the paper reports as \textbf{proved}, \(M_j = 3\) with constant hot temperatures, is also wrong as
printed. The first line of the paper's (A.12), the raw Maple coefficient of \(p_{3,1}\), equals our \(a_3\) exactly. The
``simplification'' in the next line of (A.12) silently drops \(-a_{1,1}\) (checked symbolically, claim S4, and
by the verifier, claims P3--P6). The paper's elimination was right; the hand simplification, the resulting
(A.13), and the extrapolated pattern (A.15) are wrong.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Proposition C (any exchanger law that is linear in temperatures; LMTD).} Suppose every exchanger satisfies
\(\Delta_i = \varepsilon_i\,(\theta_{h,i} - \theta_{i-1})\), where the cold-side effectiveness \(\varepsilon_i\)
depends on \(w\) and on the exchanger parameters but not on the temperatures. This holds for AMTD and for
LMTD/\(\varepsilon\)--NTU models of any flow arrangement. Put \(\varphi_i = w\,\partial\varepsilon_i/\partial w\). Then
\[\frac{\partial Q_i}{\partial w} = \Big(1 + \frac{\varphi_i}{\varepsilon_i}\Big)\Delta_i + \varepsilon_i\Big(\theta_{i-1} - \sum_{l<i}\frac{\partial Q_l}{\partial w}\Big).\]
\begin{itemize}
\item For AMTD, \(\varphi = -\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)/2\), so \(1 + \varphi/\varepsilon = \varepsilon/2\); this gives
Theorem A.
\item For a counter-current exchanger with the logarithmic mean (LMTD), \(\varepsilon = (1-E)/(1-RE)\) with
\(E = e^{-N(1-R)}\), \(N = UA/w\), \(R = w/w_h\), and \(\varphi = -N\,\partial_N\varepsilon + R\,\partial_R\varepsilon\).
Now \(\varepsilon\) and \(R = (\theta_h - \theta^{out}_h)/\Delta\) are fixed by the four terminal temperatures, and
\(N = \ln\!\big((1 - R\varepsilon)/(1-\varepsilon)\big)/(1-R)\). So the exact LMTD marginal cost is again a function of
temperatures only, but it needs the \textbf{hot outlet} temperatures as well.
\item For condensing hot streams (\(R = 0\)), \(\varphi = (1-\varepsilon)\ln(1-\varepsilon)\) and no hot outlet
temperature is needed.
\item Under AMTD the \(R\)-dependence cancels exactly: \(\varepsilon = 2/(1 + 2/N + R)\) depends on \(w\) only through
\(w k_i\). This is why the AMTD formula needs no heat capacities and no hot outlet temperatures.
\item Applied to an LMTD plant, the (corrected) AMTD formula is only an approximation. In our random tests its relative
error on the branch marginal cost has median \(\approx 9\%\), with large errors for very unbalanced exchangers,
consistent with the paper's own discussion (numerical evidence only).
\end{itemize}

\section{Proofs}

\subsection{Lemma 1 (each exchanger is an affine map of the cold temperature)}

For given \(\theta_{i-1}\), the three equations (9), (10), (12) have the unique solution
\[\theta_i = \theta_{i-1} + c_i(\theta_{h,i} - \theta_{i-1}), \qquad c_i = \frac{2}{1 + w k_i}, \qquad
   Q_i = w\,\Delta_i, \qquad \theta^{out}_{h,i} = \theta_{h,i} - Q_i/w_{h,i}.\]
For \(w_{h,i} = \infty\), take \(\theta^{out}_{h,i} = \theta_{h,i}\) and drop (10).

\emph{Proof.} Substitute \(\theta^{out}_{h,i} = \theta_{h,i} - Q_i/w_{h,i}\) and \(\theta_i = \theta_{i-1} + Q_i/w\)
into (12):
\[Q_i = UA_i\Big(\theta_{h,i} - \theta_{i-1} - \frac{Q_i}{2w} - \frac{Q_i}{2w_{h,i}}\Big)
   \iff Q_i\Big(\frac{1}{UA_i} + \frac{1}{2w} + \frac{1}{2w_{h,i}}\Big) = \theta_{h,i} - \theta_{i-1}.\]
The bracket is positive, so \(Q_i\) is unique. Dividing by \(w\) gives
\(\Delta_i = 2(\theta_{h,i} - \theta_{i-1})/(1 + w k_i)\). \(\square\)

Remarks.
\begin{itemize}
\item \(c_i\) is the temperature ratio \(c_i\) of Section 2 whenever \(\theta_{h,i} \ne \theta_{i-1}\).
\item \((UA_i, w_{h,i})\) enter only through \(k_i\). So, on the cold side, an exchanger with finite \(w_{h,i}\) is
identical to a constant-hot-temperature exchanger with \(UA' = (1/UA_i + 1/(2w_{h,i}))^{-1}\). This makes the paper's
reduction to constant hot temperatures in A.3 rigorous, and shows that the conjectured extension ``to finite heat
capacity'' adds nothing: whatever holds for constant hot temperatures holds for finite ones.
\item The paper's (A.5), \(\theta_j = \theta_{hj}/(1 - w_j(1/w_{hj} + 2/UA_j))\), has a sign error and a missing
factor 2. Section~\ref{sec:half} explains why this does not affect the controlled variable.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Lemma 2}

\[w\,\frac{\partial c_i}{\partial w} = -\frac{c_i(2-c_i)}{2}.\]
\emph{Proof.} \(\partial c_i/\partial w = -2k_i/(1+wk_i)^2\), and \(wk_i = (2-c_i)/c_i\), so
\(w\,\partial c_i/\partial w = -2\,\frac{2-c_i}{c_i}\,\frac{c_i^2}{4} = -\frac{c_i(2-c_i)}{2}\). \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition C (and so of Theorem A)}

Let \(D_i = w\,\partial\theta_i/\partial w\), with \(D_0 = 0\) because \(T_0\) is fixed.

\textbf{Step 1 (cumulative balance).} Summing (9) over \(l \le i\) gives \(\sum_{l\le i} Q_l = w\theta_i\).
Differentiating,
\[\sum_{l \le i} \frac{\partial Q_l}{\partial w} = \theta_i + D_i \qquad (i = 0,\dots,M). \tag{$*$}\]

\textbf{Step 2 (chain rule through the affine maps).} From
\(\theta_i = \theta_{i-1} + \varepsilon_i(\theta_{h,i} - \theta_{i-1})\) with \(\theta_{h,i}\) fixed,
\[D_i = (1-\varepsilon_i)D_{i-1} + \varphi_i(\theta_{h,i} - \theta_{i-1}) = (1-\varepsilon_i)D_{i-1} + \frac{\varphi_i}{\varepsilon_i}\Delta_i .\]
For \(\theta_{h,i} = \theta_{i-1}\), read \(\frac{\varphi_i}{\varepsilon_i}\Delta_i\) as
\(\varphi_i(\theta_{h,i}-\theta_{i-1}) = 0\).

\textbf{Step 3.} Taking the difference of \((*)\) at \(i\) and \(i-1\),
\[\frac{\partial Q_i}{\partial w} = \Delta_i + D_i - D_{i-1} = \Big(1 + \frac{\varphi_i}{\varepsilon_i}\Big)\Delta_i - \varepsilon_i D_{i-1}.\]
Eliminating \(D_{i-1}\) with \((*)\) at \(i-1\), namely \(D_{i-1} = \sum_{l<i}\partial Q_l/\partial w - \theta_{i-1}\),
gives Proposition C.

\textbf{AMTD.} With \(\varepsilon_i = c_i\), Lemma 2 gives \(1 + \varphi_i/c_i = c_i/2\), so
\[\frac{\partial Q_i}{\partial w} = \frac{c_i}{2}\Big(\Delta_i + 2\theta_{i-1} - 2\sum_{l<i}\frac{\partial Q_l}{\partial w}\Big)
   = \frac{c_i}{2}\Big(\theta_i + \theta_{i-1} - \sum_{l<i} 2\frac{\partial Q_l}{\partial w}\Big).\]
Setting \(a_i := 2\,\partial Q_i/\partial w\) gives exactly the recursion of Theorem A, which determines the \(a_i\)
uniquely from \(a_1\) on. The statements about \(J_j\) follow from \(J_j = -\sum_i p_{i,j} Q_{i,j}\). Since
\(A_i = \sum_{l\le i} a_l\), we get \(A_i = A_{i-1} + c_i(\theta_i + \theta_{i-1} - A_{i-1})\), which is the affine form.
\(\square\)

\textbf{Corollary B, the recursion for \(d_i\).}
\(a^P_i - a_i = c_i\big(\sum_{l<i} a_l - a^P_{i-1}\big) = c_i\big(A_{i-2} + a_{i-1} - a^P_{i-1}\big) = c_i(A_{i-2} - d_{i-1})\),
with \(d_1 = 0\) (both equal \(c_1\theta_1\)) and \(d_2 = c_2(A_0 - d_1) = 0\).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Exact counterexample (\(M_1 = 3\), \(M_2 = 1\), prices 1)}{Exact counterexample (M1 = 3, M2 = 1, prices 1)}}\label{sec:cex}

Produced by \texttt{scripts/counterexample\_exact.py} (which also checks it and rewrites
\texttt{certificates/counterexample.json}); the JSON file is re-checked from its raw parameters alone by the verifier's
\texttt{verification/v2\_exact.py} (claims X1a--X1f). All values are exact rationals.

\textbf{Data.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \(T_0 = 20\) °C, \(w_0 = 100\) kW/K, split \(w_1 = w_2 = 50\) kW/K.
\item Branch 1: \(UA = (56, 34, 28)\) kW/K, \(w_h = (88, 51, 78)\) kW/K, \(\theta_h = (48, 52, 60)\) K, i.e.~hot
inlets at 68, 72 and 80 °C.
\item Branch 2: \(UA_{1,2} = 200\), \(w_{h1,2} = 50\), and
\[\theta_{h1,2} = \frac{13184889712830714450}{136131266865364681} \approx 96.854 \text{ K}.\]
This value is chosen as \(T^P_{J,1}/c^2\) with \(c = 4/5\) (Corollary B, item 3).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What the operating point satisfies.}
\begin{itemize}
\item It is physical: in every exchanger the cold stream is heated, the cold outlet stays below the hot inlet, the hot
outlet stays above the cold inlet, and \(0 < c_i < 1\). The largest ratio of terminal temperature differences is 1.64,
so in every exchanger AMTD exceeds LMTD by at most about 2\% (the model itself is AMTD by Assumption 3).
\item Branch-1 temperatures: \(\theta_1 \approx 28.623\), \(\theta_2 \approx 38.123\), \(\theta_3 \approx 46.517\).
\item The paper's controlled variable vanishes exactly: \(T_{J,1} - T_{J,2} = 0\), with
\(T_{J,1} = T_{J,2} \approx 61.98671\).
\item The exact gradient of the recovered heat, computed by exact forward-mode differentiation of the raw model (no
formula used), is
\[\frac{\mathrm{d}Q_{tot}}{\mathrm{d}w_1}\Big|_{w_2 = w_0 - w_1} = -\frac{4972391424}{1518467647} \approx -3.2746 \ne 0.\]
This equals \((T^*_{J,1} - T^*_{J,2})/2 = -c_3a_1/2\), with \(T^*_{J,1} \approx 55.43749\).
\item The split \(w_1' = 99/2\) recovers strictly more heat: by about 1.47 kW, exactly.
\end{itemize}

So the point where the paper's controller settles is not optimal. Numerically (illustration only), the true optimum is
\(w_1^\star \approx 47.504\) (\(u^\star \approx 0.4750\) instead of 0.5), and the loss is \(\approx 4.08\) kW
(0.066\% of 6204 kW). The loss is small because the optimum is flat; the split error is about 5\% of \(w_0\).

\subsection{The paper's own validation case (Appendix B)}\label{sec:appB}

\texttt{scripts/reproduce\_appendixB.py} (and, separately, \texttt{verification/v4\_appendixB.py}) uses Table B.6:
\(M_1 = 4\), \(M_2 = 1\), prices 1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.5 / 1.5. All functions are exact rational functions of
\(u = w_1/w_0\), and real roots are isolated exactly.
\begin{itemize}
\item (B1) The corrected controlled variable equals \(-\frac{2}{w_0}\,\mathrm{d}J/\mathrm{d}u\) identically.
\item (B2) \(\mathrm{d}J/\mathrm{d}u\) has a unique root in \((0,1)\): \(u_{opt} \in [0.7141194663, 0.7141194665]\),
with \(J = -3938.8167\) and \(T_{end} = 158.77375\) °C. This matches the paper's ``Optimized'' column (0.7141, \(-3.9388\),
158.7738).
\item (B3) The paper's controlled variable (B.1), with the misprints of the printed (B.1) corrected, vanishes at
\(u_P \in [0.7167062190, 0.7167062200] \ne u_{opt}\), where \(\mathrm{d}J/\mathrm{d}u \ne 0\). This is an exact
\(M_1 = 4\) counterexample to Theorem 3 on the paper's own data. Its only other root in \((0,1)\),
\(u \approx 0.0028\), lies in the unphysical AMTD region \(c_{1,1} > 1\). With all prices set to 1 (Theorem 2), the
paper's controlled variable vanishes at \(u \approx 0.72505\) while the optimum is at \(u \approx 0.72267\)
(verifier's claim B4).
\end{itemize}

The paper reports \(u = 0.7152\) for ``Equal Jäschke Temp.''. We could not reproduce this number from (B.1), with or
without the printing typos (\(\theta^2_{h1,1}\) for \(\theta^2_{1,1}\), and \(\theta_{h3,1}-\theta_{2,1}\) for
\(\theta_{h2,1}-\theta_{1,1}\) inside the \(p_{3,1}\) term; with the typos the root moves to \(u \approx 0.904\)). The
reported end temperature 158.7741 °C is what the AMTD model gives at \(u = 0.7152\), but neither the paper's controlled
variable (value \(\approx 0.168\)) nor the corrected one (\(\approx -0.119\)) vanishes there, so the origin of the
value 0.7152 is unknown. What the exact computation does show is that the paper's own controlled variable, evaluated
without any round-off, vanishes \(0.0026\) away from the optimum. A split discrepancy of this order is therefore a
consequence of the recursion error (Corollary B), not of round-off; the paper attributed the discrepancy in end
temperature to ``the flatness of the optimum and numerical round-off errors'' and concluded that the results ``strongly
suggest that the conjecture is true''. The cost function is too flat at the optimum (it changes by \(5\times10^{-6}\)
relative between \(u_{opt}\) and \(u_P\)) to discriminate between the two formulas.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Computer algebra (\texttt{scripts/verify\_symbolic.py}, python-flint)}{Computer algebra (scripts/verify\_symbolic.py, python-flint)}}

\begin{itemize}
\item The raw model of Section 1 is solved by Cramer's rule in \(\mathbb{Q}(w, UA_i, w_{h,i}, \theta_{h,i})\) with fully
generic symbols, and \(\partial Q_i/\partial w\) is computed symbolically.
\item \textbf{S1:} it equals \(a_i/2\) (corrected recursion) as an identity of rational functions for every

constant-hot-temperature exchanger with \(UA' = (1/UA + 1/(2w_h))^{-1}\). This is the symbolic form of the remark after

\subsection{Domain of validity}

\begin{itemize}
\item Theorem A is an identity of rational functions. It holds wherever the model is defined (\(1 + wk_i \ne 0\),
automatically true for positive parameters). There is no sign restriction: it also holds for exchangers that cool the
branch, and in the unphysical AMTD regime \(c_i > 1\) (small \(w\)).
\item The temperature-only expression of \(c_i\) needs \(\theta_{h,i} \ne \theta_{i-1}\). At
\(\theta_{h,i} = \theta_{i-1}\) the exchanger transfers no heat, yet
\(\partial Q_i/\partial w = a_i/2 = \tfrac{c_i}{2}(2\theta_{i-1} - A_{i-1})\) is generally nonzero, with
\(c_i = 2/(1+wk_i)\) no longer measurable. So the paper's regularisation in §6.6.1 (setting \(a_{i,j} = 0\) near this
singularity) is itself only approximate.
\item The model assumes that the hot-side data (\(\theta_{h,i}, w_{h,i}, UA_i\)) do not depend on the split, i.e.~no
stream crossovers between branches. This is the same restriction as in the paper (§6.5).
\item With LMTD and finite hot heat capacities, \(\varphi_i/\varepsilon_i\) depends on \(R_i\) as well as on
\(\varepsilon_i\). We have not proved that no exact formula in cold temperatures and hot inlet temperatures alone
exists, but Proposition C gives an exact one that also uses the hot outlet temperatures. In the paper's §5 simulations
the AMTD-based controlled variable is a (good) heuristic for LMTD plants. For \(M_j \ge 3\) the corrected formula should
be used.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The factor \(-\tfrac12\)}{The factor -1/2}}\label{sec:half}

\begin{itemize}
\item Literally, (16) and (19) are off by the factor \(-\tfrac12\) even for \(M_j = 1\). For a single exchanger the
correct relation is \(\partial J_j/\partial w_j = -\theta_{1,j}^2/(2\theta_{h1,j})\).
\item The sign comes from \(J = -\sum pQ\), written as ``\(\partial J_j/\partial w_j = p_j\,\partial Q_j/\partial w_j\)''
in (A.6). The 2 comes from the mis-solved (A.5).
\item The factor is the same for every branch, so the optimality condition ``equal Jäschke temperatures'' is unaffected.
We regard this as a normalisation slip, not as part of the conjecture. Taken literally, however, (16) and (19) are false
for every \(M_j\), including the cases \(M_j = 1, 2\) that the paper proves.
\end{itemize}
