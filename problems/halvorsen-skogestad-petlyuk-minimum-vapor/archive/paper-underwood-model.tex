\begin{paperabstract}
Halvorsen and Skogestad (Ind.\ Eng.\ Chem.\ Res.\ 2003) conjectured that the generalized Petlyuk arrangement
(fully thermally coupled, FTC) needs less vapor than any other adiabatic distillation arrangement without internal
heat exchange, for the separation of an ideal $N$-component feed into pure products. They did not define the class
of competitors, and they proved the minimum-vapor formula of the Petlyuk arrangement itself (the highest peak of the
$V_{\min}$ diagram) only for the extreme peaks. We take as competitors all networks of two-section columns with
interval streams, side draws, thermal couplings or heat exchangers on any link and heaters or coolers at any node; they
contain Agrawal's regular-column configurations (152 for $N=4$, 6128 for $N=5$). Each column obeys the
infinite-stage Underwood model of Tumbalam Gooty, Agrawal and Tawarmalani, without reverse flows. We prove: (i) in the
strict form of the model, in which every column with two or more (nominally) distributing components runs exactly at
its Underwood vapor flow, every
network needs a total vapor generation of at least $\max_j H_j$, the reboiler-referenced highest peak, for every $N$;
the FTC attains this value, so the conjecture holds in this class and model; (ii) in the permissive form used for global
optimization, which also lets a column carry excess vapor, the same bound holds for the regular-column configurations
with $N\le4$, for every network and every $N$ when the highest peak is the first or the last one, for every regular-column
configuration and every $N$ whose mixed sub-network for the highest peak has no merge node (under a non-degeneracy
assumption), and for the FTC itself for every $N$, which proves the Halvorsen--Skogestad formula for the minimum vapor of
the generalized Petlyuk arrangement in this model (without reverse flows). Conversely, the permissive bound fails for
$N=5$: the independent verification found a five-component configuration whose permissive minimum lies below
$\max_jH_j$ (relative gap $5\cdot10^{-4}$, existence of the exact point proved by an interval Krawczyk test). It uses
excess vapor in a column in which two components distribute, which no infinite-stage column with pure products can
carry, so it is an artifact of the permissive formulation, and the restriction to the strict model is essential. The proofs combine an exact cut identity for the
Underwood flux $\Lam_s(\theta_j)=V_s-\sum_p\alpha_p w_{s,p}/(\alpha_p-\theta_j)$ with a sign rule for each column and,
for one four-component family, an ordering of recoveries. Columns with several feed stages, stream splitters,
recycles and reverse flows are outside the class; the conjecture remains open there, and at degenerate operating
points (a nominally distributing component absent from one product) the strict model is more restrictive than
infinite-stage physics (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\end{paperabstract}

\section{The question}

I.~J.~Halvorsen and S.~Skogestad, \emph{Minimum energy consumption in multicomponent distillation. 3. More than three
products and generalized Petlyuk arrangements}, Ind.\ Eng.\ Chem.\ Res.\ 42 (2003) 616--629~\autocite{HS3}. The
abstract states:
\begin{openquote}
``We also conjecture that the minimum energy requirement for the generalized extended Petlyuk arrangement is lower than
the minimum-energy requirement for any distillation configuration when we consider conventional adiabatic sections and
no internal heat exchange.''
\end{openquote}
Section~6.3 (p.~626) makes the assumptions explicit and records the status:
\begin{openquote}
``We assume constant relative volatilities, constant molar flows, constant pressure, and no internal heat integration.
Then the generalized adiabatic Petlyuk arrangement has the lowest need for vaporization compared to any other adiabatic
distillation arrangement for separation of an arbitrary feed mixture into its pure components. We have not presented a
complete proof, so the above conclusion is a conjecture.''
\end{openquote}
The number of stages is infinite; an adiabatic section is ``a column section with constant molar flows and no heat
exchange along the section''. Even the minimum vapor of the Petlyuk arrangement itself was left partly open
(Section~5.3, p.~624):
\begin{openquote}
``We have not carried out a detailed proof for the general N-component M-product case for other than the far left and
right peaks.''
\end{openquote}
For three components, Fidkowski and Kr\'olikowski~\autocite{FK87} compared the Petlyuk column with side strippers,
side rectifiers and the direct and indirect sequences in closed form. Global MINLP studies of the Agrawal group find
the FTC value optimal on the instances they solve~\autocite{TGAT,Nallasivam2016}, and later papers state the property
as known; we found no proof (novelty check in \texttt{references.md}). Counterexamples are known only when
condensers and reboilers exchange heat~\autocite{Mathew2019}, which the conjecture excludes.

Halvorsen and Skogestad do not define ``any adiabatic distillation arrangement''. A theorem must fix the class, and
the model that computes the minimum vapor of each member. We use networks of two-section columns with interval
streams (Section~\ref{sec:setting}), which contain the regular-column configurations of Agrawal and
co-workers~\autocite{SA10,TGAT}, and the infinite-stage Underwood model of Tumbalam Gooty, Agrawal and
Tawarmalani~\autocite{TGAT}. Our results in that setting:

\begin{theorem}[main results]\label{thm:summary}
Let $\alpha_1>\dots>\alpha_N>0$, $z_p>0$, $\sum z_p=1$ and $q\in\mathbb R$. Let $\theta_j\in(\alpha_{j+1},\alpha_j)$ be the
roots of $\sum_p\alpha_pz_p/(\alpha_p-\theta)=1-q$ and $H_j=\sum_{p>j}\alpha_pz_p/(\theta_j-\alpha_p)$ ($1\le j\le N-1$).
Let $R$ denote the total vapor generated by all heat exchangers (reboilers) of an arrangement.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Strict model, every $N$.) At every feasible operating point of every two-section column network
(Definition~\ref{def:network}), in particular of every regular-column configuration, $R\ge\max_j H_j$.
\item (Permissive model.) The same inequality holds at every feasible point (a) for every regular-column configuration
when $N\le4$; (b) for every network and every $N$, for $j=1$ and $j=N-1$; (c) for the FTC configuration and every $N$,
for all $j$; (d) for every regular-column configuration and every $N$, for every $j$ such that no mixed stream
(one containing $j$ and $j+1$) is produced by two columns, under a non-degeneracy assumption (N).
\item (Achievability.) The FTC configuration has an operating point, feasible in both models, with $R=\max_jH_j$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the generalized Petlyuk (FTC) arrangement minimizes total vapor generation among all two-section column
networks for every $N$ in the strict model, and among all regular-column configurations for $N\le4$ in the permissive
model; its minimum is exactly $\VFTC:=\max_jH_j$, the highest peak of the $V_{\min}$ diagram referred to the reboiler,
for every $N$ in both models.
\end{theorem}

Part~1 is Theorem~\ref{thm:strict}, part~2 is Theorems~\ref{thm:extreme}, \ref{thm:ftc}, \ref{thm:N4} and~\ref{thm:G3}, part~3 is
Theorem~\ref{thm:achieve}. Part~2 does not extend to all configurations: for $N=5$ the permissive model has a
configuration and data with minimum $R<\max_jH_j$ (Section~\ref{sec:cex}, found during the independent
verification). With $P_j^{\mathrm T}=\sum_{p\le j}\alpha_pz_p/(\alpha_p-\theta_j)$ (the $j$-th peak of the
$V_{\min}$ diagram, top vapor of a two-product column performing the sharp split $\{1..j\}|\{j+1..N\}$), the feed
equation gives $H_j=P_j^{\mathrm T}-(1-q)$, so $\VFTC$ is the value of~\autocite{HS3}, eq.~(8), referred to the
reboiler. The two standing hypotheses of the model, stated in Section~\ref{sec:model}, are: no reverse flows (net
component flows of fixed sign in every section), and a single feed with pure, saturated-liquid products. The argument
is entirely analytic; the scripts of Section~\ref{sec:computer} check identities, search for counterexamples and
confirm the case analysis, but no step of the proofs depends on floating-point computation.

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Networks of two-section columns}
A \emph{stream} is an interval $[i,m]=\{i,\dots,m\}$ of components ($i\le m$); $[p,p]$ is the pure product $p$ and
$[1,N]$ the feed.

\begin{definition}[two-section column network]\label{def:network}
A network consists of the $N$ pure-product nodes and a finite set of \emph{columns}. A column $c$ has a label $[i,m]$
($i<m$) and a split $[i,k]/[l,m]$ with $i\le k<m$, $i<l\le m$ and $l\le k+1$ (components $l,\dots,k$ distribute). It has
one feed stage, between a top and a bottom adiabatic section of infinitely many stages. Its top section delivers the
distillate $\dist(c)=[i,k]$ to one node with that label (a column labelled $[i,k]$, or the pure product $i$ if
$k=i$), and its bottom section delivers the residue $\res(c)=[l,m]$ to one node labelled $[l,m]$. Exactly one column,
the \emph{feed column}, is labelled $[1,N]$ and receives the process feed; every other column receives at least one
stream. A node may receive any number of streams (from several producers, as a distillate or as a residue), and
several columns may carry the same label. Each link is a thermal coupling (the vapor and liquid of the producing
section are exchanged directly at the receiving node) or passes through a heat exchanger, and every node may carry an
external heat duty: vapor generated (a reboiler or heater, counted in $R$) or vapor condensed (a condenser or
cooler). Pure products are withdrawn as saturated liquid.
\end{definition}

Labels shrink strictly along links, so a network is acyclic and every column is reached from the feed column. Not
covered: columns with several feed stages (intermediate sections between feeds), stream splitters that divide one
section product among several columns, recycles, non-interval intermediate streams, splits in which a product keeps
every component of the column's feed (excluded by $k<m$ and $l>i$), streams delivered to a node whose label differs
from the stream's label, and the reverse flows excluded in Section~\ref{sec:model}.

\emph{Regular-column configurations}~\autocite{SA10,TGAT} are the networks with at most one column per label, in which
the distillate of $[i,m]$ is $[i,k]$ for the largest $k<m$ such that a column $[i,k]$ exists (or $k=i$), the residue is
$[l,m]$ for the smallest such $l>i$, a stream produced by one column is transferred by a thermal coupling or by a
condenser (distillate; TG: a partial condenser removing the reflux, the stream leaving as saturated vapor) or a reboiler
(residue; the stream leaving as saturated liquid), a stream produced by two columns is a side draw (thermal
couplings), and a pure product is withdrawn through a total condenser or a reboiler, or as a liquid side draw between
the two sections that produce it. There are 8, 152 and 6128 such configurations for $N=3,4,5$ (transfer choices
included). The \emph{FTC} (generalized Petlyuk) configuration contains every mixture stream, all links being thermal
couplings or side draws; it has one reboiler and one condenser.

\subsection{The Underwood model}\label{sec:model}
Constant relative volatilities, constant molar overflow, constant pressure, infinitely many stages in every section.
For a column $c$ with split $[i,k]/[l,m]$: net upward component flows $d_p\ge0$ in the top section ($i\le p\le k$), net
downward flows $b_p\ge0$ in the bottom section ($l\le p\le m$), net feed $f_p=d_p[p\le k]+b_p[p\ge l]$ (the feed $z$ for
the feed column, otherwise the sum of the incoming flows), vapor flows $V_T,V_B\ge0$ with $V_T-V_B=U_c$, where $U_c$ is
the net vapor entering the feed stage: $1-q$ for the feed column; otherwise the vapor brought by the incoming
distillate links, minus the vapor drawn by thermally coupled residue links, plus the vapor generated minus the vapor
condensed at the node. In a regular-column configuration with TG's conventions, a thermally coupled or side-draw
distillate link brings $V_T(\text{producer})$, a condensed one $D=V_T-L_T$, a coupled residue link draws
$V_B(\text{producer})$ and a reboiled one draws nothing. Put
\[g_T(x)=\sum_{p\le k}\frac{\alpha_pd_p}{\alpha_p-x},\qquad g_B(x)=-\sum_{p\ge l}\frac{\alpha_pb_p}{\alpha_p-x}.\]
For every $r\in\{l-1,\dots,k\}$ there is a root $\theta_{c,r}\in[\alpha_{r+1},\alpha_r]$ of the feed equation
$\sum_p\alpha_pf_p/(\alpha_p-\theta_{c,r})=U_c$, and a threshold vapor $Y_T$ (with $Y_B=Y_T-U_c$) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TG}
\begin{gathered}
g_T(\theta_{c,r})\le Y_T\quad(r=l-1,\ r=k),\qquad g_T(\theta_{c,r})=Y_T\quad(l\le r\le k-1),\\
0\le Y_T\le V_T,\qquad 0\le Y_B\le V_B .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
This is TG's model~\autocite{TGAT} (constraints (A18)--(A23) of the arXiv version, including the equalities at the
interior roots which, TG note, are needed for the correct degrees of freedom); we call it the \emph{permissive} model.
(Nallasivam et al.~\autocite{Nallasivam2016} use a closely related formulation: an inequality $V_T\ge g_T(\theta)$ at
every root together with explicit recovery-ordering constraints, instead of TG's equalities at the interior roots.
The results below are proved for TG's formulation.) The \emph{strict} model adds $Y_T=V_T$ whenever the column has an interior root
($l\le k-1$): the column then runs exactly at the Underwood vapor of each of its interior roots, as an infinite-stage
column does whenever the components $l,\dots,k$ actually distribute (then these roots are active): ``we may apply the common roots being in the range of volatilities for the
components distributed to both ends. We denote these roots active roots. If we have any active roots then
$V=V_{\min}$'' (Halvorsen~\autocite{HalvorsenThesis}, \S4.2.1; see also \S3.2.3--3.2.4). The permissive model also admits columns carrying vapor above their threshold (``a column may be operated
above its threshold vapor requirement to supply the vapor to another column''~\autocite{TGAT}), physically a vapor
bypass or a finite-stage effect. Strict-feasible points are permissive-feasible.

\emph{Degenerate operating points} (added by the verifier). Both models fix the split of each column through the
network, so the strict model imposes $Y_T=V_T$ whenever two or more components are \emph{nominally} distributing,
whether or not they appear in both products. At a point where every component $l,\dots,k$ has a positive flow in
both products the interior roots are active and the strict model is exactly the infinite-stage behaviour. At a
degenerate point, where a nominally distributing component is absent from one product (e.g.\ $d_C=0$ in a split
ABCD$\to$ABC/BCD), an infinite-stage column is governed by its actual distribution: Halvorsen counts a component as
distributing only if it ``appears in both products, or is exactly at the limit of becoming distributing''
(\autocite{HalvorsenThesis}, \S3.2.4), so with fewer than two distributing components no root is active and the column
may physically carry vapor above its Underwood value, which the strict model forbids; TG's equalities at the nominal
interior roots are then not physically required either. Relabelling the split does not help in general, because the
delivered stream would no longer carry the label of its receiver (Definition~\ref{def:network}). Such points are not
covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:strict}; the theorems are statements about the model as defined. (The results that use
only the sign rule, Lemma~\ref{lem:sign}, rest on the inequality $V_T\ge g_T(\theta)$ at a common root, which holds at
any infinite-stage operating point with nonnegative flows; we expect, but have not written out, their extension to
degenerate points.)

At the roots, $g_T-g_B=U_c$, hence
$g_B(\theta_{c,r})\le Y_B$ for $r=l-1,k$ with equality for interior $r$.

\emph{Standing hypotheses.} (H1) No reverse flows: $d_p\ge0$ and $b_p\ge0$ in every section. This is part of the
model of~\autocite{TGAT,Nallasivam2016}: the conditions~\eqref{eq:TG} express Underwood's root interlacing for top
sections with $w\ge0$ and bottom sections with $w\le0$, and with reverse flows the roots can leave their intervals
(Halvorsen~\autocite{HalvorsenThesis}, \S4.3.3--4.3.4). Reverse flows do occur in directly coupled columns operated
away from the preferred split, e.g.\ in a Petlyuk prefractionator whose top liquid carries a component that the top
section would otherwise remove (\emph{ibid.}, \S4.3.3), so (H1) is a genuine restriction of the operating points,
not only of the configurations.
The cut identity below needs no sign assumption, but the column lemmas use (H1) through the monotonicity of the
Underwood functions; whether reverse flows could lower $R$ is not addressed here. (H2) A single feed, and pure
products withdrawn as saturated liquid.

The objective is $R$, the vapor generated by all heat exchangers (reboilers on products and on transfer streams,
heaters at nodes).

\subsection{Peaks and regions}
Fix $j\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}$ and write $\theta=\theta_j$. A stream, column or node with label $[i,m]$ is \emph{light}
if $m\le j$, \emph{heavy} if $i\ge j+1$, \emph{mixed} if $i\le j<m$. Producers of a mixed column are mixed (their labels
contain its label); the distillate of a mixed column is light or mixed (it contains $i\le j$), its residue mixed or
heavy; the sections of a light (heavy) column join light (heavy) nodes. A \emph{light exit} is the top section of a
mixed column whose distillate is light; a \emph{heavy exit} is the bottom section of a mixed column whose residue is
heavy.

\section{The Underwood flux and the cut identity}

For a section $s$ with vapor flow $V_s$ and upward net component flows $w_s$ ($w=d$ in a top section, $w=-b$ in a
bottom section), define the \emph{Underwood flux}
\[\Lam_s(x)=V_s-\sum_p\frac{\alpha_pw_{s,p}}{\alpha_p-x}.\]
For a column $c$: $\Lam_{c,T}=V_T-g_T$, $\Lam_{c,B}=V_B-g_B$, and its \emph{feed function}
$\Lam_{c,F}(x)=U_c-\sum_p\alpha_pf_p/(\alpha_p-x)$ satisfies $\Lam_{c,T}-\Lam_{c,B}=\Lam_{c,F}$.

\begin{lemma}[monotonicity]\label{lem:mono}
On any interval free of poles, $\Lam_{c,T}$ and $\Lam_{c,F}$ are nonincreasing and $\Lam_{c,B}$ is nondecreasing;
$\Lam_{c,F}$ is strictly decreasing if some $f_p>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The derivatives are $-\sum\alpha_pd_p/(\alpha_p-x)^2$, $-\sum\alpha_pf_p/(\alpha_p-x)^2$ and $+\sum\alpha_pb_p/(\alpha_p-x)^2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Nodes and the identity}
The \emph{nodes} of a network are its columns and its pure products: a column node gathers the feed stage of the
column, the heat exchangers on its incoming links and its external duty; a product node gathers the withdrawal point
and its heat exchanger (or the side draw). Every section joins the node of its column to the node that receives its
distillate (top section) or its residue (bottom section). We orient sections upward and write $s\colon X\to Y$ when the
bottom end of $s$ is in $X$ and its top end in $Y$.

\begin{proposition}[cut identity]\label{prop:cut}
Let $U$ be a set of nodes containing every light pure product and no heavy pure product, $U^c$ its complement. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cut}
R-H_j=R(U)+Q(U^c)+\sum_{s\colon U^c\to U}\Lam_s(\theta_j)-\sum_{s\colon U\to U^c}\Lam_s(\theta_j),
\end{equation}
where $R(U)$ is the reboiler duty (vapor generated) located in $U$ and $Q(U^c)\ge0$ the condenser duty (vapor
condensed) located in $U^c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\Lam_s(x)$ is linear in $(V_s,w_s)$. At a node, constant molar overflow and the absence of heat exchange give
conservation of the total vapor flow and of the net component flows, except for: the feed, which injects vapor $1-q$
and components $z$; a heat exchanger, which converts liquid into vapor (reboiler, duty $Q_r$) or vapor into liquid
(condenser, duty $Q_c$); a liquid product withdrawal of $z'_p$ of component $p$. A section whose top end is at the
node brings the flux $\Lam_s$ into it; a section whose bottom end is at the node takes $\Lam_s$ out of it (its vapor
leaves upward, its liquid arrives, its net flow $w_s$ leaves upward). Hence, at every node,
\[\sum_{s\text{ ending at the node}}\Lam_s(x)-\sum_{s\text{ starting at the node}}\Lam_s(x)
=Q_c-Q_r-\Lam^{\mathrm{feed}}(x)-\frac{\alpha_pz'_p}{\alpha_p-x},\]
with $\Lam^{\mathrm{feed}}(x)=(1-q)-\sum_p\alpha_pz_p/(\alpha_p-x)$ at the feed column only and the last term at product
nodes only. (Agent C verified this identity in exact rational arithmetic on all configurations for $N\le5$, with
arbitrary junction duties.) At $x=\theta_j$, $\Lam^{\mathrm{feed}}=0$ by the
definition of $\theta_j$. Sum the balances over the nodes of $U^c$: sections inside $U^c$ cancel, every heavy product
withdraws $\alpha_pz_p/(\theta_j-\alpha_p)$, in total $H_j$, and no light product lies in $U^c$. This gives
$R(U^c)=H_j+Q(U^c)+\sum_{U^c\to U}\Lam_s-\sum_{U\to U^c}\Lam_s$; add $R(U)$.
\end{proof}

The canonical choice $U=$ light streams gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lightcut}
R-H_j=R(\text{light})+Q(\text{mixed})+Q(\text{heavy})+\sum_{\text{light exits}}\Lam_s(\theta_j),
\end{equation}
and $U=$ light $\cup$ mixed streams gives
$R-H_j=R(\text{light})+R(\text{mixed})+Q(\text{heavy})+\sum_{\text{heavy exits}}\Lam_s(\theta_j)$.
So $R\ge H_j$ as soon as the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:cut} is nonnegative for one admissible $U$; conversely, the
inequality is tight on open sets of parameters for many configurations (Agent C's computations), so no strict
inequality can be expected.

\section{Column lemmas}

For a mixed column $c$ put $A_c=\Lam_{c,T}(\theta_j)$, $B_c=\Lam_{c,B}(\theta_j)$ and $a_c=\Lam_{c,F}(\theta_j)=A_c-B_c$.

\begin{lemma}[sign rule]\label{lem:sign}
For every mixed column, in both models: $A_c\ge\min(0,a_c)$, equivalently $B_c\ge\min(0,-a_c)$. In particular
$A_c$ and $B_c$ are never both negative, $a_c\ge0\Rightarrow A_c\ge0$ and $a_c\le0\Rightarrow B_c\ge0$. Moreover,
if $k<j$ then $A_c\ge0$, and if $l>j+1$ then $B_c\ge0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let the split be $[i,k]/[l,m]$; $\eqref{eq:TG}$ and $Y\le V$ give $\Lam_{c,T}(\theta_{c,r})\ge0$ and
$\Lam_{c,B}(\theta_{c,r})\ge0$ for $r\in\{l-1,\dots,k\}$. The values are finite: if $\theta_{c,r}$ is an endpoint
$\alpha_{r'}$, the feed equation forces $f_{r'}=0$, hence $d_{r'}=b_{r'}=0$.

\emph{Case $l-1\le j\le k$.} If all flows vanish the claim is trivial. Otherwise $\Lam_{c,F}$ is strictly decreasing on
$[\alpha_{j+1},\alpha_j]$ (where finite) and vanishes at $\theta_{c,j}$. If $a_c\ge0$ then $\theta_j\le\theta_{c,j}$, and
$A_c\ge\Lam_{c,T}(\theta_{c,j})\ge0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:mono}. If $a_c\le0$ then $\theta_j\ge\theta_{c,j}$ and
$B_c\ge\Lam_{c,B}(\theta_{c,j})\ge0$, so $A_c=a_c+B_c\ge a_c$.

\emph{Case $k<j$.} The top section contains only components $p\le k$, so $\Lam_{c,T}$ has no pole in
$(-\infty,\theta_{c,k}]$; since $\theta_j<\alpha_j\le\alpha_{k+1}\le\theta_{c,k}$, $A_c\ge\Lam_{c,T}(\theta_{c,k})\ge0$; and if
$a_c\le0$, $B_c=A_c-a_c\ge0$. \emph{Case $l>j+1$}: symmetric, with $\theta_{c,l-1}<\alpha_{j+1}<\theta_j$ and $\Lam_{c,B}$
nondecreasing on $[\theta_{c,l-1},\infty)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[strict model: interior split]\label{lem:interior}
In the strict model, if both the distillate and the residue of a mixed column are mixed ($l\le j\le k-1$), then $A_c$
and $B_c$ are never both positive.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$j$ is an interior root of $c$, so $V_T=Y_T=g_T(\theta_{c,j})$: $\Lam_{c,T}(\theta_{c,j})=\Lam_{c,B}(\theta_{c,j})=0$. If
$\theta_j\le\theta_{c,j}$ then $B_c\le0$; if $\theta_j\ge\theta_{c,j}$ then $A_c\le0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mono}).
\end{proof}

For the feed column $a_{[1,N]}=\Lam^{\mathrm{feed}}(\theta_j)=0$, so $A=B\ge0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:sign}.
\emph{Propagation.} By the node balance of Proposition~\ref{prop:cut}, a column $c$ other than the feed column has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prop}
a_c=\sum_{P:\ \dist(P)=c}A_P-\sum_{P':\ \res(P')=c}B_{P'}+\rho_c-\kappa_c,
\end{equation}
where $\rho_c\ge0$ is the vapor generated and $\kappa_c\ge0$ the vapor condensed at the node of $c$ (in TG's
convention: $\kappa_c=L_T(P)$ for a condensed distillate link, $\rho_c=V_B(P')$ for a reboiled residue link, $0$ for
couplings and side draws).

\section{The strict model: all \texorpdfstring{$N$}{N}}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:strict}
In the strict model, every feasible operating point of every two-section column network satisfies $R\ge H_j$ for
every $j$.
\end{theorem}

The argument is due to the computation agent (Agent C) and was checked by the theory agent; a standalone
machine check of its combinatorial core is described in Section~\ref{sec:computer}.

\begin{proof}
Fix $j$ and an operating point. Build the \emph{sign graph}: for every section $s\colon x\to y$, an arc $x\to y$ if
$\Lam_s(\theta_j)>0$ and an arc $y\to x$ if $\Lam_s(\theta_j)<0$. Let $U$ be the set of nodes reachable from the light
nodes (light nodes included). If $U$ contains no heavy node, then every section $s\colon U^c\to U$ has
$\Lam_s\ge0$ (otherwise its bottom end would be reachable) and every section $s\colon U\to U^c$ has $\Lam_s\le0$; so all
terms of~\eqref{eq:cut} are nonnegative and $R\ge H_j$.

We show by induction on the length of a shortest path from the light nodes that every reached mixed node $y$
satisfies (i) $B_y\ge0$ and (ii) $A_y\le0$ if $\dist(y)$ is mixed, and that no heavy node is reached. The sections
incident to a mixed column node $y$ are its top section (to the node receiving $\dist(y)$), its bottom section (from
the node receiving $\res(y)$) and the sections of its producers ending at $y$; we write $\dist(y)$, $\res(y)$ also for
these nodes. Let $y$ be reached by an arc from an already reached node $z$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $z=\dist(y)$ (light or mixed), arc down the top section of $y$: then $A_y<0$, so $B_y\ge0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sign}; (i) and (ii) hold.
\item $z=\res(y)$, arc up the bottom section of $y$: then $B_y>0$. The node $z$ is mixed (a residue of a mixed column
is not light, and no heavy node has been reached). If $\dist(y)$ is mixed, both products of $y$ are mixed and
Lemma~\ref{lem:interior} gives $A_y\le0$. So (i) and (ii) hold.
\item $z$ a producer of $y$ (necessarily mixed): an arc up the top section of $z$ needs $A_z>0$, excluded by (ii) for
$z$ since $\dist(z)=y$ is mixed; an arc down the bottom section of $z$ needs $B_z<0$, excluded by (i) for $z$.
\end{itemize}
A heavy node $y$ could only be entered from a mixed column $c$ with $\res(c)=y$, down the bottom section of $c$, which
needs $B_c<0$, against (i); no section joins a light node to a heavy node. Hence $U$ contains no heavy node.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[necessary condition for a tie; Agent C]\label{cor:tie}
In the strict model, every feasible point of every network satisfies $R-H_j\ge R(\text{light})+Q(\text{heavy})$ for every
$j$, where $R(\text{light})$ is the vapor generated at nodes with labels inside $[1,j]$ and $Q(\text{heavy})$ the vapor
condensed at nodes with labels inside $[j+1,N]$. Hence, if $H_{j^*}$ is the strict maximum of the peaks, a network can
attain $\VFTC$ only if it has no reboiler or heater with positive duty at a node inside $[1,j^*]$ and no condenser or
cooler with positive duty at a node inside $[j^*+1,N]$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:strict}, $U$ contains the light nodes, $U^c$ the heavy ones, and all flux terms
of~\eqref{eq:cut} are nonnegative; so $R-H_j\ge R(U)+Q(U^c)\ge R(\text{light})+Q(\text{heavy})$.
\end{proof}
\noindent In Agent C's runs ($N=4$, $q=1$), 2717 of 2720 observed ties satisfy this condition; the three exceptions are
near-ties (relative gap $\sim10^{-9}$) caused by trace components.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:hx}
The proof uses only Lemmas~\ref{lem:sign}--\ref{lem:interior} column by column, the interval labels and the
identity~\eqref{eq:cut}. Heat duties never create arcs and enter~\eqref{eq:cut} with nonnegative signs, which is why
the theorem holds for the whole class of Definition~\ref{def:network}: any thermal state of the transferred streams,
heaters or coolers at any node, side draws merging any number of links, several columns with the same label, any
$q$, and no regularity rule. Hypothesis (H1) enters through Lemma~\ref{lem:mono}.
\end{remark}

\section{The permissive model}

In the permissive model Lemma~\ref{lem:interior} fails: a column with an interior root may run above its threshold,
with $A_c=B_c=V_T-Y_T>0$. The reachability argument then leaves possible ``bad paths''; Agent C's exact enumeration
finds none for $N=3$, exactly one family for $N=4$ (family~K below), 70 paths in 48 basic configurations for $N=5$ and
5795 in 2300 for $N=6$.

\subsection{Extreme peaks and paths}

\begin{lemma}[path]\label{lem:path}
In a network in which every mixed column has at most one mixed child (the node receiving its distillate or its
residue), $R\ge H_j$ in both models.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every mixed column is reached from the feed column through mixed producers, and each has at most one mixed child;
hence the mixed columns form a path $c_0\to c_1\to\dots\to c_n$ ($c_0$ the feed column), and $c_{t+1}$ has the single
producer $c_t$. Write $a_t,A_t,B_t$ for $c_t$, $\rho_t,\kappa_t\ge0$ for the vapor generated and condensed at the node of
$c_{t+1}$, $T_t$ for the sum of $A$ over the light exits among $c_t,\dots,c_n$ and $K_t=\sum_{t'\ge t}\kappa_{t'}$. We claim
$T_t\ge\min(0,a_t)-K_t$. For $t=n$: $T_n=A_n\ge\min(0,a_n)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sign}; the top section of $c_n$ is a light
exit). If $c_{t+1}=\dist(c_t)$: $T_t=T_{t+1}$ and $a_{t+1}=A_t+\rho_t-\kappa_t\ge\min(0,a_t)-\kappa_t$. If
$c_{t+1}=\res(c_t)$, the top of $c_t$ is a light exit and $a_{t+1}=-B_t+\rho_t-\kappa_t$; then
$T_t=A_t+T_{t+1}\ge A_t+\min(0,a_{t+1})-K_{t+1}$, which is $\ge A_t-K_{t+1}\ge\min(0,a_t)-K_t$ if $a_{t+1}\ge0$, and
$\ge A_t-B_t-\kappa_t-K_{t+1}=a_t-K_t$ otherwise. With $a_0=0$: $\sum_{\text{light exits}}\Lam_s=T_0\ge-K_0\ge-Q(\text{mixed})$,
and~\eqref{eq:lightcut} gives $R\ge H_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[extreme peaks]\label{thm:extreme}
In both models, for every $N$ and every two-section column network, $R\ge H_1$ and $R\ge H_{N-1}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $j=1$ the mixed columns are labelled $[1,m]$, $m\ge2$; their residues are heavy, so each has at most one mixed
child (its distillate) and Lemma~\ref{lem:path} applies. For $j=N-1$ the mixed columns are labelled $[i,N]$ and
their distillates are light.
\end{proof}

\noindent For $N=3$ both peaks are extreme: the conjecture holds in both models for all eight ternary configurations
and every $q$. For any $N$, the conjecture holds in both models whenever the highest peak is $H_1$ or $H_{N-1}$.

\subsection{Recovery ordering and merge-free mixed networks}\label{sec:G}

The results of this subsection and their proofs are due to the general-$N$ agent (Agent G,
\texttt{general/general-N.md}); they hold for regular-column configurations under the non-degeneracy assumption
\begin{quote}
(N) every column receives a positive net flow of every component of its label.
\end{quote}

\begin{lemma}[recovery ordering]\label{lem:G1}
Let $c$ be a column with split $[i,k]/[l,m]$, at a point satisfying~\eqref{eq:TG}, with $f_r>0$ for all $r\in[i,m]$.
Put $d_r:=f_r$ for $r<l$ and $d_r:=0$ for $r>k$. Then $d_p/f_p\ge d_q/f_q$ for all $p<q$ in $[i,m]$; equivalently
$d_pb_q\ge d_qb_p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Only $l\le p<q\le k$ needs proof. Let $D=Y_T-g_T$, $\tilde B(x)=Y_B+\sum_{r\ge l}\alpha_rb_r/(\alpha_r-x)$ and
$F=D-\tilde B=U_c-\sum_r\alpha_rf_r/(\alpha_r-x)$. By~\eqref{eq:TG}, $D(\theta_{c,r})=\tilde B(\theta_{c,r})$, equal to $0$
for interior $r$ and $\ge0$ for $r\in\{l-1,k\}$. Suppose $d_p/f_p<\lambda<d_q/f_q$ with $\lambda$ different from every
$d_s/f_s$, so $0<\lambda<1$, and put $H=(1-\lambda)D+\lambda\tilde B=D-\lambda F=K_0-\sum_{s=i}^m\alpha_sc_s/(\alpha_s-x)$ with
$c_s=d_s-\lambda f_s\neq0$. The signs $\sigma_s$ of $c_s$ are $+$ for $s<l$, $-$ for $s>k$, $\sigma_p=-$, $\sigma_q=+$; the
sequence $\sigma_i,\dots,\sigma_m$ has $C\ge3$ sign changes, all between $s=l-1$ and $s=k+1$. Write
$H=P(x)/\prod_s(\alpha_s-x)$, $\deg P\le n:=m-i+1$, $P\not\equiv0$. Near $\alpha_s$, $H\approx-\alpha_sc_s/(\alpha_s-x)$, so
on $I_r=(\alpha_{r+1},\alpha_r)$, $i\le r<m$, $H$ has sign $\sigma_{r+1}$ at the left end and $-\sigma_r$ at the right end. If
$\sigma_r=\sigma_{r+1}$, $P$ has a zero in $I_r$. If $\sigma_r\neq\sigma_{r+1}$, then $l-1\le r\le k$, both ends of $I_r$ have
the same sign, and $H(\theta_{c,r})=0$ ($r$ interior) or $H(\theta_{c,r})\ge0$ with both ends negative ($r=l-1$: ends
$\sigma_l=-$ and $-\sigma_{l-1}=-$; $r=k$: ends $\sigma_{k+1}=-$ and $-\sigma_k=-$); either way $P$ has at least two zeros in
$I_r$, counted with multiplicity. In total $P$ has at least $(n-1-C)+2C\ge n+2$ zeros, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:recovery} below is the case $(p,q)=(\mathrm B,\mathrm C)$ of the feed column of K, with a direct proof
that does not need (N).

\emph{Light and heavy parts.} A function $y(x)=U-\sum_p\alpha_pw_p/(\alpha_p-x)$ with $w\ge0$ (nonincreasing on $I_j$) is
written $y=U-G_y+K_y$ with $G_y(x)=\sum_{p\le j}\alpha_pw_p/(\alpha_p-x)$ (nondecreasing on $I_j$) and
$K_y(x)=\sum_{p>j}\alpha_pw_p/(x-\alpha_p)$ (nonincreasing). This applies to the feed function $a_c:=\Lam_{c,F}$, to
$\Lam_{c,T}$, to $-\Lam_{c,B}$ and to the fluxes carried by the links into a mixed column: $y_e=\Lam_{P,T}-\kappa_e$ for a
distillate link from $P$, $y_e=-\Lam_{P',B}+\rho_e$ for a residue link from $P'$ ($\kappa_e$, $\rho_e$ the duties of a
condenser or reboiler on the link), so that $a_c=\sum_ey_e$ as functions on $I_j$ (cf.~\eqref{eq:prop}). Under (N)
each of them has a unique zero $\zeta_y$ in $I_j$; let $\theta^c$ be the zero of $a_c$ and
\[\Gamma(y):=G_y(\zeta_y)-G_y(\theta_j).\]
For the feed column $\Gamma(a)=0$, since $\zeta=\theta_j$. A mixed column is of type (a) if its distillate is light and
its residue heavy, (b) light and mixed, (c) mixed and heavy, (d) mixed and mixed; in type (d), $j$ is an interior root
and $\Lam_{c,T}(\theta^c)=\Lam_{c,B}(\theta^c)=e_c:=V_T-Y_T\ge0$.

\begin{lemma}[local inequality]\label{lem:G4}
Under (N), let $c$ be a mixed column, $y_1$ and $y_2$ the fluxes of its links to its mixed distillate and residue
(when these are mixed), $\kappa$ the condenser duty on the first of these links (if any), and $\ell_c=A_c$ if $c$ is of
type (a) or (b), $\ell_c=0$ otherwise. Then $\ell_c+\kappa+\sum_e\Gamma(y_e)\ge\Gamma(a_c)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $G=G_{a_c}=G^T+G^B$, with $G^T$, $G^B$ the light parts of $\Lam_{c,T}$ and $-\Lam_{c,B}$, and
$s_c=\Lam_{c,T}(\theta^c)=\Lam_{c,B}(\theta^c)\ge0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sign}).
(a) The top section carries light components only: $\Lam_{c,T}=V_T-G$, so $\ell_c=s_c+G(\theta^c)-G(\theta_j)=s_c+\Gamma(a_c)$.
(b) The top carries only light components, the bottom all heavy ones. With $z=\zeta_{y_2}$ ($\Lam_{c,B}(z)=\rho$),
$\ell_c+\Gamma(y_2)-\Gamma(a_c)=s_c-[G^B(\theta^c)-G^B(z)]$. If $z\ge\theta^c$ the bracket is $\le0$. If $z<\theta^c$,
$s_c-\rho=\Lam_{c,B}(\theta^c)-\Lam_{c,B}(z)=[G^B(\theta^c)-G^B(z)]+[K^B(z)-K^B(\theta^c)]$ and the second bracket is $\ge0$.
(c) Symmetric: with $z=\zeta_{y_1}$, $\kappa+\Gamma(y_1)-\Gamma(a_c)=\kappa+G(z)-G(\theta^c)$; if $z<\theta^c$,
$\kappa-s_c=\Lam_{c,T}(z)-\Lam_{c,T}(\theta^c)\ge G(\theta^c)-G(z)$.
(d) With $e=e_c$, $z_1=\zeta_{y_1}$ ($\Lam_{c,T}(z_1)=\kappa$), $z_2=\zeta_{y_2}$ ($\Lam_{c,B}(z_2)=\rho$), the claim reads
$X:=[G^T(z_1)-G^T(\theta^c)]+\kappa-[G^B(\theta^c)-G^B(z_2)]\ge0$. If $\kappa\le e$ and $\rho\le e$, then
$z_1\ge\theta^c\ge z_2$ and
\[\begin{aligned}
e-\kappa&=(z_1-\theta^c)\sum_px_p, & x_p&=\frac{\alpha_pd_p}{|\alpha_p-\theta^c||\alpha_p-z_1|},\\
e-\rho&=(\theta^c-z_2)\sum_py_p, & y_p&=\frac{\alpha_pb_p}{|\alpha_p-\theta^c||\alpha_p-z_2|},
\end{aligned}\]
$G^T(z_1)-G^T(\theta^c)=\sigma_1(e-\kappa)$ and $G^B(\theta^c)-G^B(z_2)=\sigma_2(e-\rho)$, where $\sigma_1=X_L/(X_L+X_H)$,
$\sigma_2=Y_L/(Y_L+Y_H)$, $X_L=\sum_{p\le j}x_p$, $X_H=\sum_{p>j}x_p$, and similarly for $Y$. For $p\le j<q$,
$x_py_q-x_qy_p$ is a positive multiple of $d_pb_q\,v^1_pv^2_q-d_qb_p\,v^1_qv^2_p$ with $v^t_s=1/|\alpha_s-z_t|$; since
$v^1_p/v^2_p=(\alpha_p-z_2)/(\alpha_p-z_1)\ge1\ge(z_2-\alpha_q)/(z_1-\alpha_q)=v^1_q/v^2_q$ and $d_pb_q\ge d_qb_p$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:G1}), it is $\ge0$; summing, $X_LY_H\ge X_HY_L$, i.e.\ $\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2$. Hence
$X=\sigma_1e+(1-\sigma_1)\kappa-\sigma_2(e-\rho)\ge(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)e\ge0$. If $\kappa>e$: $z_1<\theta^c$ and
$\kappa-e=\Lam_{c,T}(z_1)-\Lam_{c,T}(\theta^c)\ge G^T(\theta^c)-G^T(z_1)$, so the first two terms of $X$ add up to at least
$e$, while the last bracket is $\le e-\rho\le e$ if $\rho\le e$ and $\le0$ if $\rho>e$. If $\kappa\le e<\rho$: the first
bracket is $\ge0$ and the last $\le0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[merge-free mixed networks; Agent G]\label{thm:G3}
In the permissive model, under (N), if no mixed column of a regular-column configuration has two producers (no
\emph{merge node} in the mixed sub-network for $j$), then $R\ge H_j$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The mixed columns form a tree rooted at the feed column; each non-root mixed column $c'$ has a single link $e$, and
$a_{c'}=y_e$. Let $L(c)$ be the sum of $\ell$ over the mixed columns of the subtree of $c$ plus the condenser duties
on the links inside it. From the leaves up, Lemma~\ref{lem:G4} and $L(c')\ge\Gamma(a_{c'})=\Gamma(y_e)$ give
$L(c)\ge\Gamma(a_c)$. At the root, $\sum_{\text{light exits}}\Lam_s(\theta_j)+Q(\text{mixed})=L([1,N])\ge0$; conclude
with~\eqref{eq:lightcut}.
\end{proof}

\noindent For regular-column configurations satisfying (N), Theorem~\ref{thm:G3} contains Lemma~\ref{lem:path} and Lemma~\ref{lem:K}. Agent G also shows that
$R\ge H_j$ holds at every permissive point at which no merge node descends from a type-(d) column whose excess
satisfies $\Lam_{c,T}(\theta_j)>0$ and $\Lam_{c,B}(\theta_j)>0$ (possible only if $e_c>0$), by combining the sign rule (with superadditivity of $\min(0,\cdot)$
at merges) above those columns and Lemma~\ref{lem:G4} below them (\texttt{general/general-N.md}, Theorem G6). The
mechanism that defeats a general proof: $\Gamma$ is not superadditive at merge nodes (mixing a vapor-rich and a
liquid-rich feed lowers the threshold vapor).

\subsection{A five-component counterexample in the permissive model}\label{sec:cex}

This subsection was added by the independent verifier. The bound $R\ge\VFTC$ is false in the permissive model for
$N=5$. Take the regular-column configuration (basic configuration 161 of the Shah--Agrawal space, all transfers by
thermal coupling, B, C and D withdrawn as liquid side draws)
\[\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{ABCDE}\to\mathrm{ABCD}/\mathrm{BCDE},\quad \mathrm{ABCD}\to\mathrm{ABC}/\mathrm{BCD},\quad \mathrm{BCDE}\to\mathrm{BCD}/\mathrm{CDE},\\
&\mathrm{ABC}\to\mathrm{AB}/\mathrm{BC},\quad \mathrm{BCD}\to\mathrm{BC}/\mathrm D,\quad \mathrm{CDE}\to\mathrm C/\mathrm{DE},\\
&\mathrm{AB}\to\mathrm A/\mathrm B,\quad \mathrm{BC}\to\mathrm B/\mathrm C,\quad \mathrm{DE}\to\mathrm D/\mathrm E,
\end{aligned}\]
a saturated-vapor feed ($q=0$),
\[\alpha=\Bigl(\tfrac{10675204}{742583},\tfrac{5824255}{854876},\tfrac{529647}{171259},\tfrac{1673613}{789461},1\Bigr)
\approx(14.3758,\,6.8130,\,3.0927,\,2.1199,\,1),\]
and the rational feed
$z\approx(0.19036,0.18832,0.17089,0.03888,0.41156)$ chosen so that the feed roots are the rationals
$\theta\approx(12.3250,5.5538,2.5300,1.9504)$ (exact values in the file
\texttt{permissive\_counterexample\_N5.json} of the folder \texttt{verification/}).
Then $H\approx(0.33442,0.32912,0.46994,0.43302)$ and $\VFTC=H_3\approx0.469941$, while the permissive model has a
feasible point with $R\approx0.469685$, i.e.\ $R/\VFTC\approx0.99946$, at which every net flow is at least $10^{-6}$ (so
assumption (N) holds) and every inequality of~\eqref{eq:TG} holds with slack at least $10^{-6}$. That an exact feasible
point exists within $10^{-30}$ of the computed one is proved by a Krawczyk test in interval arithmetic on a square
subsystem of the equalities (\texttt{verification/krawczyk\_certificate.py}); the inequalities and $R<\VFTC$ are then
checked over the whole box in interval arithmetic.

The mechanism is the one isolated in Section~\ref{sec:G}: for $j=3$ the column BCDE is of type (d) and runs about $0.006$
above its threshold ($V_T\approx0.03015$, $Y_T\approx0.02420$) although C and D distribute in it with positive flows,
so its root at $j=3$ is active and an infinite-stage column could not carry this excess; its distillate feeds the
merge node BCD, which is not the bottom of a simple diamond. In the strict model, Theorem~\ref{thm:strict} excludes
such a point, and a multistart minimization of the same configuration at the same data gives $R/\VFTC=1$ to
$10^{-7}$. Hence the permissive (TG-literal) model is not a faithful model for the conjecture, and of the 20 open
(basic configuration, $j$) pairs at least one, (161, 3), is settled negatively.

\emph{Physical meaning.} We do not know an adiabatic arrangement with pure products that realizes this point, and
the natural candidates fail. (i) An infinite-stage column in which C and D appear in both products has an active
root and runs at its Underwood vapor (Halvorsen's rule quoted in Section~\ref{sec:model}); excess vapor at a fixed
product distribution requires fewer stages, but a section with finitely many stages cannot remove B from the residue
or E from the distillate completely, and as the impurities tend to zero both sections become infinite and the excess
is forced to zero. So the point is at best the limit of finite-stage operations with \emph{impure} intermediate
streams, which the conjecture does not compare. (ii) A vapor bypass carrying the excess from the node below BCDE to
the node above it, or a separate section with $V=L$ between these nodes, transports components (E upward into the
node BCD, B downward, or C and D in opposite directions), whereas the permissive excess leaves every net flow
unchanged; it therefore realizes a different point, outside Definition~\ref{def:network}. The excess-vapor device of
TG is physically sound for columns in which at most one component distributes (then $V$ above the threshold does not
change the distribution), and the strict model keeps exactly that freedom. We therefore regard the point as an
artifact of the permissive formulation, not as a counterexample to a physical reading of the conjecture; it does,
however, show that a global optimizer applied to TG's model can return configurations below the FTC value.

\subsection{The FTC configuration, every \texorpdfstring{$N$}{N}}

\begin{theorem}[minimum vapor of the generalized Petlyuk arrangement]\label{thm:ftc}
In both models, every feasible point of the FTC configuration satisfies $R\ge\max_jH_j$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $j$. The mixed streams form the grid $(i,m)$, $1\le i\le j<m\le N$; the column $(i,m)$ splits $[i,m]$ into
$[i,m-1]$ and $[i+1,m]$. Row $i$ is the chain $(i,N)\to(i,N-1)\to\dots\to(i,j+1)$ of distillate links, ending in the
light exit of $(i,j+1)$; the column $(i,m)$ also receives the flux $x^{(i)}_m:=-B_{(i-1,m)}$ from row $i-1$ ($x^{(1)}\equiv0$;
all links are couplings or side draws, so~\eqref{eq:prop} has no duty terms). Put $y^{(i)}_m=-B_{(i,m)}$ and
$Y^{(i)}_m=\sum_{m'\ge m}y^{(i)}_{m'}$, $X^{(i)}_m=\sum_{m'\ge m}x^{(i)}_{m'}=Y^{(i-1)}_m$.

\emph{Claim: if $X^{(i)}_m\le0$ for all $m$, then $Y^{(i)}_m\le0$ for all $m$.} Along the row,
$a_{(i,N)}=x_N$ and $a_{(i,m)}=x_m+A_{(i,m+1)}$. We show $A_{(i,m)}\ge X_m$ by descending induction:
$A_{(i,N)}\ge\min(0,x_N)=X_N$; if $A_{(i,m+1)}\ge X_{m+1}$ then $a_{(i,m)}\ge X_m$ and
$A_{(i,m)}\ge\min(0,a_{(i,m)})\ge\min(0,X_m)=X_m$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sign}). Since $y_m=a_{(i,m)}-A_{(i,m)}$, telescoping gives
$Y_m=X_m-A_{(i,m)}\le0$.

Row 1 has $X\equiv0$ (the feed column has $a=0$). By induction $Y^{(j)}_{j+1}\le0$; but $-Y^{(j)}_{j+1}$ is the sum of
$\Lam$ over the heavy exits (the bottom sections of the columns $(j,m)$). The second form of~\eqref{eq:lightcut}
gives $R-H_j\ge-Y^{(j)}_{j+1}\ge0$.
\end{proof}

This proves, in the Underwood model, the statement of~\autocite{HS3} \S3.1 and \S5 (``the minimum-energy solution for
a generalized Petlyuk arrangement \dots is given by the highest peak in the $V_{\min}$ diagram'') for every $N$, the
attainment being Theorem~\ref{thm:achieve}. When a grid node is missing, the rows no longer absorb the residue fluxes
of the previous row, and the induction breaks; family~K is the smallest instance.

\subsection{Four components}

For $N=4$, $j\in\{1,3\}$ is Theorem~\ref{thm:extreme}. For $j=2$ the mixed streams are among ABCD, ABC, BCD, BC
(A, B, C, D $=$ components 1--4). ABC is present iff it is the distillate of ABCD, BCD iff it is its residue, and BC, if
present, is the residue of ABC and the distillate of BCD whenever these are present. Hence: if ABC or BCD is absent,
the mixed streams form a path (Lemma~\ref{lem:path}); if ABC, BCD and BC are present, Lemma~\ref{lem:diamond}; the
remaining family is
\[\begin{aligned}\mathrm K=\{&\mathrm{ABCD}\to\mathrm{ABC}/\mathrm{BCD},\ \mathrm{ABC}\to\mathrm{AB}/\mathrm C,\ \mathrm{BCD}\to\mathrm B/\mathrm{CD},\\
&\mathrm{AB}\to\mathrm A/\mathrm B,\ \mathrm{CD}\to\mathrm C/\mathrm D\}\end{aligned}\]
with its 16 coupling/exchanger variants (B and C are liquid side draws).

\begin{lemma}[diamond]\label{lem:diamond}
If ABC, BCD and BC are present ($N=4$, $j=2$), then $R\ge H_2$ in both models.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Columns $0=$ ABCD, $1=$ ABC, $2=$ BCD, $3=$ BC; light exits: tops of 1 and 3. $a_0=0$ gives $A_0=B_0\ge0$;
$a_1=A_0-\kappa_1\ge-\kappa_1$, $a_2=-B_0+\rho_2$, $a_3=-B_1+A_2$ (side draw). By Lemma~\ref{lem:sign},
$A_1\ge\min(0,a_1)\ge-\kappa_1$. If $a_3\ge0$ then $A_3\ge0$ and $A_1+A_3\ge-\kappa_1$. Otherwise $A_3\ge a_3$ and
$A_1+A_3\ge A_1-B_1+A_2=a_1+A_2$; if $a_2\ge0$ then $A_2\ge0$ and the sum is $\ge-\kappa_1$; if $a_2<0$ then $A_2\ge a_2$ and
the sum is $\ge A_0-\kappa_1-B_0+\rho_2\ge-\kappa_1$. Conclude with~\eqref{eq:lightcut}, $Q(\text{mixed})=\kappa_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[recovery ordering]\label{lem:recovery}
If the feed column splits ABCD into ABC/BCD (permissive model), its top recoveries satisfy $d_B/z_B>d_C/z_C$. In
particular $d_B>0$ and $b_C=z_C-d_C>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The roots of the feed column are $\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3$, $\theta_2$ interior. By~\eqref{eq:TG},
$\Phi_1:=g_T(\theta_2)-g_T(\theta_1)\ge0$ and $\Phi_3:=g_T(\theta_2)-g_T(\theta_3)\ge0$, with $d=(z_A,d_B,d_C,0)$.
On the diagonal $d_B=rz_B$, $d_C=rz_C$ ($0\le r\le1$), the feed equation gives
$g_T(\theta_t)=r(1-q)+(1-r)\mathcal A(\theta_t)+r\mathcal D(\theta_t)$ with $\mathcal A(x)=\alpha_Az_A/(\alpha_A-x)$ increasing and
$\mathcal D(x)=\alpha_Dz_D/(x-\alpha_D)$ decreasing on $(\alpha_D,\alpha_A)$. Thus $\Phi_1=-(1-r)A_{12}+rD_{12}$ and
$\Phi_3=(1-r)A_{32}-rD_{32}$ with $A_{12}=\mathcal A(\theta_1)-\mathcal A(\theta_2)$, $D_{12}=\mathcal D(\theta_2)-\mathcal D(\theta_1)$,
$A_{32}=\mathcal A(\theta_2)-\mathcal A(\theta_3)$, $D_{32}=\mathcal D(\theta_3)-\mathcal D(\theta_2)$, all positive, and
\[\frac{A_{12}D_{32}}{A_{32}D_{12}}=\frac{(\alpha_A-\theta_3)(\theta_1-\alpha_D)}{(\alpha_A-\theta_1)(\theta_3-\alpha_D)}>1 .\]
For $r=0$, $\Phi_1<0$; for $r=1$, $\Phi_3<0$; for $0<r<1$, $\Phi_1,\Phi_3\ge0$ would give $A_{12}D_{32}\le A_{32}D_{12}$. So no
feasible point lies on the diagonal. Moreover $\partial\Phi_1/\partial d_B=\alpha_B/(\alpha_B-\theta_2)+\alpha_B/(\theta_1-\alpha_B)>0$ and
$\partial\Phi_3/\partial d_B=\alpha_B/(\alpha_B-\theta_2)-\alpha_B/(\alpha_B-\theta_3)>0$: lowering $d_B$ below $z_Bd_C/z_C$ lowers both, so
$d_B/z_B\le d_C/z_C$ is infeasible. As the inequality is strict, $d_B>0$ and $d_C<z_C$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[family K]\label{lem:K}
For every variant of family K, $R\ge H_2$ in both models.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Columns $0=$ ABCD, $1=$ ABC, $2=$ BCD; light exits: tops of 1 and 2. Since $\theta_2$ is an interior root of column 0,
$A_0=B_0=V_T-Y_T=:e\ge0$. Column 1 is fed with $d=(z_A,d_B,d_C)$ and $\Lam_{1,F}=\Lam_{0,T}-\kappa_1$; its split AB/C has the
single root $\rho_1:=\theta_{1,2}\in I_2$, with $\Lam_{1,T}(\rho_1)\ge0$. Column 2 is fed with $b=(b_B,b_C,z_D)$,
$\Lam_{2,F}=-\Lam_{0,B}+\rho_2'$ ($\rho_2'$ the reboiler duty on the link, $0$ for a coupling), root
$\rho_2:=\theta_{2,2}$, $\Lam_{2,T}(\rho_2)\ge0$. Let $\Delta_p(x)=1/(\alpha_p-x)-1/(\alpha_p-\theta_2)$, whose sign is that of
$x-\theta_2$ for every $p$. Then
\[\begin{aligned}
A_1&\ge\sum_{p=A,B}\alpha_pd_p\Delta_p(\rho_1), & e-\kappa_1&=\sum_{p=A,B,C}\alpha_pd_p\Delta_p(\rho_1),\\
A_2&\ge\alpha_Bb_B\Delta_B(\rho_2), & \rho_2'-e&=\sum_{p=B,C,D}\alpha_pb_p\Delta_p(\rho_2),
\end{aligned}\]
the equalities because the feed functions vanish at $\rho_1,\rho_2$ and equal $e-\kappa_1$, $-e+\rho_2'$ at $\theta_2$. By
\eqref{eq:lightcut} it suffices to show $A_1+A_2+\kappa_1\ge0$. If $\rho_1,\rho_2\ge\theta_2$: $A_1,A_2\ge0$. If
$\rho_1\le\theta_2\le\rho_2$: $A_2\ge0$ and $A_1+\kappa_1\ge e-\alpha_Cd_C\Delta_C(\rho_1)\ge e$. If $\rho_1,\rho_2\le\theta_2$:
$A_1+\kappa_1\ge e$ and $A_2\ge\rho_2'-e-\alpha_Cb_C\Delta_C(\rho_2)-\alpha_Dz_D\Delta_D(\rho_2)\ge-e$. In the remaining case
$\rho_1\ge\theta_2\ge\rho_2$ put
\[X=\alpha_Cd_C\Delta_C(\rho_1),\ Y=\alpha_Bd_B\Delta_B(\rho_1),\ Z=\alpha_Bb_B|\Delta_B(\rho_2)|,\ W=\alpha_Cb_C|\Delta_C(\rho_2)|,\]
all $\ge0$, with $X+Y\le e-\kappa_1\le e$, $Z+W\le e-\rho_2'\le e$ and $A_1+A_2+\kappa_1\ge e-X-Z$. Now
$X/(X+Y)+Z/(Z+W)-1=(XZ-YW)/((X+Y)(Z+W))$, so $XZ\le YW$ implies $X+Z\le e$ (if $X+Y=0$ or $Z+W=0$ this is immediate).
Finally $XZ\le YW$ reads
\[\frac{d_Cb_B}{d_Bb_C}\le\frac{\mathrm{Rat}(\rho_2)}{\mathrm{Rat}(\rho_1)},\qquad
\mathrm{Rat}(x):=\frac{\Delta_C(x)}{\Delta_B(x)}=\frac{(\alpha_B-x)(\alpha_B-\theta_2)}{(x-\alpha_C)(\theta_2-\alpha_C)} .\]
The left side is $\le1$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:recovery} ($d_Bz_C\ge d_Cz_B\iff d_Bb_C\ge d_Cb_B$); $\mathrm{Rat}$ is decreasing on
$I_2$ and $\rho_1\ge\rho_2$, so the right side is $\ge1$. (By Lemma~\ref{lem:recovery}, $d_B>0$ and $b_C>0$, so $\rho_1<\alpha_B$
and $\rho_2>\alpha_C$ and all quantities are finite.)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$N=4$]\label{thm:N4}
In both models, every feasible point of every one of the 152 four-component configurations satisfies $R\ge\max(H_1,H_2,H_3)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:extreme}, Lemmas~\ref{lem:path}, \ref{lem:diamond} and~\ref{lem:K}, by the case distinction above.
\end{proof}
\noindent Agent G also proves, for every $N$ and under (N), that $R\ge H_j$ in the permissive model whenever every merge
node of the mixed sub-network is the bottom of a \emph{simple diamond}: a type-(d) column whose distillate column is
of type (b) and whose residue column is of type (c), both feeding the merge node (\texttt{general/general-N.md},
Theorem G8). With Theorem~\ref{thm:G3} this gives an independent proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:N4} under (N); the proof above
needs no non-degeneracy assumption.

\section{Attainment by the FTC}\label{sec:achieve}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:achieve}
For every $N$, $\alpha$, $z>0$ and $q\in\mathbb R$, the FTC configuration has an operating point, feasible in the strict
(hence in the permissive) model, with $R=\max_jH_j$; all its flows are positive.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Preferred split.} Let a column $[i,m]$, $m-i\ge2$, have positive feed $f$ and net vapor $U$ such that
$\theta_i,\dots,\theta_{m-1}$ are roots of its feed equation. Define $V_T$ and $d$ by
\[\alpha_pd_p=V_T\,\frac{\prod_{r=i}^{m-1}(\alpha_p-\theta_r)}{\prod_{p'\in[i,m-1],p'\ne p}(\alpha_p-\alpha_{p'})}\quad(i\le p\le m-1),\]
where the factor $V_T$ is fixed by the condition $d_i=f_i$ (the formula for $p=i$), and $b=f-d$ ($d_m:=0$), $V_B=V_T-U$. Partial fractions give $V_T-g_T(x)=V_T\prod_r(x-\theta_r)/\prod_{p=i}^{m-1}(x-\alpha_p)$, so
$g_T(\theta_r)=V_T$ for all $r$; with the feed equation, $g_B(\theta_r)=V_B$, and since $V_B-g_B$ has its poles at
$\alpha_{i+1},\dots,\alpha_m$, $V_B-g_B(x)=V_B\prod_r(x-\theta_r)/\prod_{p=i+1}^{m}(x-\alpha_p)$ ($V_B\ne0$, otherwise $b\equiv0$,
contradicting $b_m=f_m>0$). Counting signs of the factors, every $d_p$ has the sign of $V_T$ and every $b_p$ the sign of
$V_B$; as $d_i=f_i>0$ and $b_m=f_m>0$, all are positive. At $x=0$: $L_T=V_T-\sum d=V_T\prod_r\theta_r/\prod_{p<m}\alpha_p$ and
$L_B=V_B+\sum b=V_B\prod_r\theta_r/\prod_{p>i}\alpha_p$, so $0<L_T<V_T$ and $L_B>V_B>0$. The column satisfies~\eqref{eq:TG}
with $Y_T=V_T$ and all roots active.

\emph{Construction.} Process the columns by decreasing length. The feed column ($f=z$, $U=1-q$) and every column
$[i,m]$ with $m-i\ge2$ are put at the preferred split. A child $[i,m]$ receives $d$ from $[i,m+1]$ and $b$ from
$[i-1,m]$ (those present), with $U=V_T[i,m+1]-V_B[i-1,m]$; since both producers have every common root active,
$\theta_i,\dots,\theta_{m-1}$ are roots of its feed equation (carry-over~\autocite{CW89,HS2}), and its feed is positive.
A binary column $[r,r+1]$ gets $d_r=f_r$, $b_{r+1}=f_{r+1}$, $V_T=\alpha_rf_r/(\alpha_r-\theta_r)+e_r$,
$V_B=\alpha_{r+1}f_{r+1}/(\theta_r-\alpha_{r+1})+e_r$ with $e_r:=\max_sH_s-H_r\ge0$; it has no interior root, so excess
vapor is allowed in both models.

\emph{Side draws.} It remains to check $V_T[p,p+1]=V_B[p-1,p]$ at the side draws $p=2,\dots,N-1$ (product purities hold
by construction). Let $\delta_p$ be the vapor lost at the node of $p$ (inflow from below minus outflow above), treated in
Proposition~\ref{prop:cut} as a condenser duty (or a reboiler duty $-\delta_p$). For the split $r$, the light exits are
the tops of $[i,r+1]$, $i\le r$; for $i<r$ the column is at its preferred split with root $\theta_r$ active, so
$\Lam=0$; for $i=r$, $\Lam=e_r$. The only other heat duty is the reboiler of product $N$. Proposition~\ref{prop:cut}
with $U=$ light streams gives $R-\sum_{p=r+1}^{N-1}\delta_p=H_r+e_r=\max_sH_s$ for $r=1,\dots,N-1$. For $r=N-1$ this is
$R=\max_sH_s$, and successive differences give $\delta_{N-1}=\dots=\delta_2=0$.
\end{proof}

\noindent The construction is the one of~\autocite{HS3} \S3 (every internal column at its preferred split); the
excess vapor is placed in the binary columns, whose children are pure products, so that no carried-over root moves.

\section{Computer verification and numerical evidence}\label{sec:computer}

No proof above depends on a computation. The scripts check the algebra, cross-check the case analysis and search
for counterexamples (environment: WSL Fedora, Python 3 with sympy, mpmath, scipy, pyomo, ipopt; paths relative to
the problem folder).
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{theory/scripts/check\_algebra.py} (sympy, exact): the identities used in Lemmas~\ref{lem:recovery},
\ref{lem:K} and Theorem~\ref{thm:achieve}. PASS.
\item \texttt{theory/scripts/check\_ftc\_achievability.py} (mpmath, 80 digits): builds the point of
Theorem~\ref{thm:achieve} for 200 random instances for each $N=3,\dots,8$ ($q\in[-1,2]$) and checks every constraint of
the strict model, the side draws and $R=\max H_j$ to $10^{-50}$. PASS.
\item \texttt{theory/scripts/signcheck.py certify4}: decides, by linear programming over the sign patterns of the
inflows, which (configuration, $j$) pairs are not settled by Lemma~\ref{lem:sign}, \eqref{eq:prop} and the cut identity
alone; for $N=4$ these are exactly the 16 variants of family K at $j=2$. PASS. (For $N=5$ the sign rule alone leaves
1312 (configuration, $j$) pairs for each of $j=2,3$, in 2160 of the 6128 configurations and 48 basic configurations.
The LPs have integer data but are solved in floating point (HiGHS); the statement that the other pairs are
settled by the sign rule is therefore computer-assisted, not a hand proof.)
\item Agent C (\texttt{computation/}): the enumeration of the configuration space (8/152/6128 configurations, as in
\autocite{SA10}); an exact-arithmetic check of the cut identity on all configurations for $N\le5$; an exact enumeration of
sign-consistent light-to-heavy paths (none in the strict model for $N\le6$; family K only for $N=4$ in the permissive
model); multistart local minimization (ipopt, every accepted point re-verified a posteriori) of $R$ in the strict
model over all 152 configurations at 239 random points for $N=4$, $q=1$ (minimum ratio $R/\VFTC=1-1.4\cdot10^{-9}$),
and over all 6128 configurations at the eight test points of~\autocite{TGAT} for $N=5$ (minimum $=\VFTC$ to
$10^{-10}$ relative, the FTC attaining it); adversarial joint minimization over $(\alpha,z,q)$ and the operating point
(ratio never below 1 beyond $10^{-6}$), and targeted minimization on the permissive ``bad paths'' (family K; the
all-coupled variants of the 48 five-component families; 181 of the merge cases), never below 1; SCIP global
optimization used only as a cross-check at two points for $N=4$ (strict model, all 152 configurations, at
$\alpha=(8,4,2,1)$, $z_p=1/4$, $q=1$: SCIP and ipopt agree to $2.2\cdot10^{-9}$, and the SCIP dual bound is $\ge\VFTC$
for every non-tying configuration, while for the tying ones it reached $\VFTC(1-8\cdot10^{-6})$ at the time limit;
permissive model, first 100 configurations, at the point of the example below: agreement to $3.7\cdot10^{-9}$). Many
exact ties (for $N=4$, $q=1$, 12 to 16 configurations tie with the FTC, depending on the dominant peak; 2717 of 2720
observed ties satisfy Corollary~\ref{cor:tie}). Random searches with $q\ne1$ and a random permissive search for $N=5$
were not run. The FTC value reproduces the eight five-component optima of~\autocite{TGAT} (all eight test points have
an extreme highest peak, $j=1$ or $j=4$, so they are covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:extreme} in both models), and the
model reproduces Halvorsen's ternary table~\autocite{HalvorsenThesis} (Table~6.1). Scripts and data:
\texttt{computation/scripts/} and \texttt{computation/certificates/}.
\item Agent G (\texttt{general/scripts/}): \texttt{recovery\_order\_lp.py} (LP minimum of $d_p/f_p-d_{p+1}/f_{p+1}$
over the constraints~\eqref{eq:TG} of random columns, 1587 LPs, positive), \texttt{classify\_mixed.py} (types and merge
nodes of the mixed sub-networks for $N=4,5,6$), \texttt{gamma\_check.py} (stress test of Lemmas~\ref{lem:sign},
\ref{lem:G1}, \ref{lem:G4} at optimal and random feasible points). \texttt{theory/scripts/opencount.py} intersects the
sign-hard pairs with the pairs having a merge node ($N=5$: 864 pairs, 720 configurations; Agent C's path enumeration
gives the same 20 (basic configuration, $j$) pairs).
\item \texttt{theory/scripts/stressK.py}: minimizes $d_B/z_B-d_C/z_C$, $A_1+A_2+\kappa_1$ and $R-H_2$ over the 16 variants
of K in the permissive model: minima $+0.039$, $-2\cdot10^{-10}$ and $-2\cdot10^{-10}$ (round-off), consistent with
Lemmas~\ref{lem:recovery} and~\ref{lem:K} and showing that the bound is tight.
\item Independent verification (\texttt{verification/}, code written from the equations, sharing nothing with the
scripts above): an exact (rational) check of the node-balance form of the cut identity on 400 random networks of
Definition~\ref{def:network} (several columns per label, merges, heaters and coolers at every node, random subsets
$U$, rational evaluation points); an independent construction of the attainment point of Theorem~\ref{thm:achieve}
(60 digits, $N=3,\dots,7$, 200 instances); the FTC value at the eight test points of~\autocite{TGAT}; an independent
enumeration (3/8, 18/152, 203/6128) and minimum-vapor NLP for all 8 ternary and all 152 four-component
configurations (13 runs in the strict or the permissive model) at 9 parameter points ($q\in\{-0.3,0.3,0.4,0.5,1,1.3\}$, four of them with
the middle peak highest): Halvorsen's Table~6.1 is reproduced (1.882, 2.032, 2.072, 1.556, 1.366), no configuration
falls below $\VFTC$ beyond the feasibility tolerance ($10^{-7}$), and all 120 observed ties satisfy
Corollary~\ref{cor:tie}. For $N=5$, a multistart probe of the 20 open (basic configuration, $j$) pairs in the
permissive model (all-coupled and all-exchanger variants, 220 random parameter points with the peak $H_j$ highest and

\end{itemize}

\end{example}

\section{Scope, limitations and open questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Class.} Theorem~\ref{thm:strict} and Theorem~\ref{thm:extreme} hold for every two-section column network
(Definition~\ref{def:network}); Theorem~\ref{thm:N4} is proved for the 152 regular-column configurations with $N=4$.
The wording ``any adiabatic distillation arrangement'' of~\autocite{HS3} would also admit columns with several feed
stages (intermediate sections between feeds), stream splitters dividing one section product among several columns,
recycles, non-interval intermediate streams, splits in which one product keeps every component of the feed, streams
of different labels merged at one feed stage, and reverse net flows; none of these is covered. Heat integration
between streams and several pressure levels are excluded by the conjecture itself.
\item \emph{Reverse flows.} Hypothesis (H1) is part of the model of~\autocite{TGAT,Nallasivam2016}. The cut identity
holds without it, but Lemmas~\ref{lem:sign} and~\ref{lem:interior} use the monotonicity of Lemma~\ref{lem:mono}, and
the conditions~\eqref{eq:TG} themselves rest on Underwood's interlacing of roots, which fails with reverse
flows~\autocite{HalvorsenThesis}. Whether reverse flows could lower $R$ is open.
\item \emph{Model.} The theorems are statements about Underwood's infinite-stage model as formulated by TG, with the
split of each column fixed by the network (components $l,\dots,k$ nominally distributing, possibly with zero flow on
one side); its physical justification is that of~\autocite{HalvorsenThesis,TGAT}. At degenerate points (a nominally
distributing component absent from one product) the strict model forbids excess vapor that an infinite-stage column
could physically carry, and imposes TG's equalities at roots that are not active (Section~\ref{sec:model}); physical
operating points of this kind are not covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:strict}.
\item \emph{Permissive model, $N\ge5$.} The bound can fail only for splits $j$ whose mixed sub-network has a merge node
below a type-(d) column carrying excess vapor, when the sign rule does not settle the case. For $N=5$ these are 20
(basic configuration, $j$) pairs, i.e.\ 864 (configuration, $j$) pairs and 720 of the 6128 configurations (in 16 basic
configurations), and it does fail for the pair (161, 3) (Section~\ref{sec:cex}); the other 19 pairs are undecided
(a verifier's multistart search on their all-coupled and all-exchanger variants found no other point below
$\VFTC$). All other configurations are covered (Theorems~\ref{thm:extreme} and~\ref{thm:G3}, the latter under
(N), and the sign rule, the last by the floating-point LP classification of \texttt{signcheck.py}). Numerical searches
find no counterexample. Degenerate points (assumption (N) violated) are
not covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:G3}.
\item \emph{Products and feed.} Pure products withdrawn as saturated liquid, a single feed (H2). Nonsharp product
specifications and several feeds are not treated. (A pure product withdrawn as saturated vapor instead would only
increase the bound: in the node balance a heavy vapor product contributes $\theta_jz_p/(\theta_j-\alpha_p)$ instead of
$\alpha_pz_p/(\theta_j-\alpha_p)$, and light products do not enter~\eqref{eq:cut}.)
\end{enumerate}
