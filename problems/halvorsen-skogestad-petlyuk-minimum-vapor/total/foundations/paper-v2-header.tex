\begin{paperabstract}
Halvorsen and Skogestad (Ind.\ Eng.\ Chem.\ Res.\ 2003) conjectured that the generalized Petlyuk arrangement needs less
vaporization than any other adiabatic distillation arrangement without internal heat exchange, for an ideal feed
(constant relative volatilities and molar flows, constant pressure, infinitely many stages, pure products). We
formalize ``any arrangement'' as the class $\mathcal A$ of all finite networks of constant-molar-overflow equilibrium stages,
single-phase splitters and mixers, total condensers and total vaporizers, with one feed that may be split and
conditioned, heat duties at any stage, recycles and products of either phase; infinitely many stages and pure products
enter as a limit. We prove that at every steady state of every arrangement in $\mathcal A$, with any numbers of stages and
any purities, the total heat supplied satisfies $R\ge\sum_P\max(0,\varphi_P(\theta_j))$ for each root $\theta_j$ of the feed
equation, where $\varphi_P(\theta)=v_P-\sum_i\alpha_in_{P,i}/(\alpha_i-\theta)$ is the Underwood flux of the product stream $P$; for
products of purity $1-\varepsilon$ this gives $R\ge H_j-c_j\varepsilon$, and in the limit $R\ge\max_jH_j$, the highest peak of the
$V_{\min}$ diagram, which is the minimum vapor of the generalized Petlyuk arrangement. The proof is elementary: every
stream leaving an equilibrium stage has an Underwood flux of the sign of $-\sum_i\alpha_ix_i/(\alpha_i-\theta_j)$, $x$ the stage
liquid, so the units split into a light and a heavy side, and the energy balance of the heavy side gives the bound,
with an exact expression of the excess as three nonnegative losses. It covers stream splitting, recycles, several feed
stages, reverse flows, degenerate distributions, non-adiabatic sections and Murphree trays, which version~1 of this
paper could not treat, and it shows that the five-component point below the Petlyuk value found in version~1 in the
permissive form of Underwood's model cannot be approached by physical operation. The generalized Petlyuk arrangement
attains the bound in Underwood's infinite-stage model (version~1) and, for two components, in the finite-stage model;
for three or more components steady states exist at every prescribed flows, an isolated prefractionator is proved to
admit exact finite-stage profiles, and the closed Petlyuk network reaches pure products at $R=\max_jH_j+\delta$ in three
independent simulations, but its finite-stage attainment is not proved. Hence no arrangement of $\mathcal A$ needs less
vaporization than the Petlyuk value computed by Halvorsen and Skogestad, and the conjecture is completely proved for two
components. Version~1's results in the Underwood model, independently verified, are retained.
\end{paperabstract}
