# The class 𝒜 of adiabatic distillation arrangements and the physical model

Agent F (foundations), Claude Opus 5.5 (effort xhigh). **Version 3, 2026-10-20 14:22** (v1 13:48: definition;
v2 14:02: Murphree stages, the HS 2002 conference paper, finite-purity constant; v3: self-contained statement and
proof of the stage-level bound in §5). Reference definition of the class 𝒜 and of the physical model for the
second wave on this problem; it is self-contained. Paths are relative to the problem folder
`problems/halvorsen-skogestad-petlyuk-minimum-vapor/`; sources in `certificates/sources/` (HS3 = Halvorsen &
Skogestad 2003 Part 3, doi:10.1021/ie0108651; Th = Halvorsen's thesis, NTNU 2001:43). "Result 12" is version 1
of `paper.tex` (Underwood-model results, independently verified in `verification.md`).

Summary. The physical model is the **finite-stage, constant-molar-overflow, equilibrium-stage network** with
constant relative volatilities at constant pressure; "infinitely many stages and pure products" is the limit
ε → 0 of the infimum of the total vaporization R over steady states with product purities ≥ 1 − ε. The class 𝒜
contains every finite network of equilibrium stages, single-phase splitters and mixers, total condensers and total
vaporizers, with one process feed (which may be split and conditioned), external heat duties at any unit, recycles,
and products of either phase. It contains every arrangement HS have in mind (and more: non-adiabatic sections with
external duties). Section 5 states and proves the stage-level bound (found independently by three agents of the
second wave, N, S and F), which makes the conjecture a theorem in 𝒜 modulo attainment by the Petlyuk arrangement.

## 1. What Halvorsen and Skogestad say (sources of the definition)

- Assumptions (HS3 §6.3, p. 626): constant relative volatilities, constant molar flows, constant pressure, no
  internal heat integration, separation of an arbitrary feed mixture into its pure components; infinite number of
  stages (HS3 §1, p. 616).
- Adiabatic section (HS3 §6, p. 624, after Petlyuk et al. 1964): a column section "with constant molar flows and no
  heat exchange along the section"; HS3 §1 (p. 617): "no heat exchange along the section". Both directly coupled
  columns and "typical conventional arrangements contain adiabatic sections" (HS3 §6).
- Th §6.1.1 (pp. 170–171): a column section is "a set of connected equilibrium stages"; in non-adiabatic sections
  heat is supplied or removed at any stage; an arrangement is built from sections, reboilers and condensers;
  sections may be directly coupled by two-way liquid and vapour streams or coupled via condensers or reboilers, and
  are divided at stages with feed or side-draw streams. Minimum energy = minimum external heat supply to reboilers
  and to non-adiabatic sections; "Sometimes we use the total requirement for vaporization or vapour flow as
  measure of the energy requirement."
- Internal heat exchange (Th §6.5, pp. 188–191): heat removed in one part of the arrangement used as heat supply in
  another (across the dividing wall, sidestream condensation heating the feed, middle sections of a reversible
  arrangement). With it the external heat can go below the Petlyuk value (Th Table 6.1: 1.222, 1.181, 1.000 against
  1.366). Several pressure levels (Th §6.6) are combined with heat exchange between columns.
- HS, Distillation & Absorption 2002 (Baden-Baden), paper 6-27 ("Minimum energy and entropy requirements in
  multicomponent distillation"): states the conjecture for adiabatic arrangements at constant pressure without
  internal heat integration; the minimum energy is the external heat supply to reboilers and non-adiabatic
  sections, measured by the total vaporization; infinitely many stages are assumed "because this provides a lower
  bound on the energy requirement"; the case with internal heat exchange is called unknown.
- The objective "need for vaporization" (HS3 §6.3) = total vapor generated by heat input = total heat supply
  divided by the (constant) molar heat of vaporization.
- CMO (Th §2.6.1): with saturated-liquid enthalpy 0 and saturated-vapor enthalpy equal to a common molar heat of
  vaporization, the energy balance of any stage reduces to "vapor out − vapor in = heat input / heat of
  vaporization". This is the energy balance used at every unit below.

## 2. Data

Components 1..N (N ≥ 2), relative volatilities α_1 > α_2 > ... > α_N > 0 (constant), ideal vapor–liquid
equilibrium y_i = K_i(x) x_i with K_i(x) = α_i / S(x), S(x) = Σ_k α_k x_k. Units: molar heat of vaporization = 1,
so heat duties are measured in moles of vapor. Process feed F = 1 with composition z (all z_p > 0, Σ z_p = 1) and
thermal state q ∈ ℝ (liquid fraction; q > 1 subcooled, q < 0 superheated): its vapor equivalent is 1 − q.
Feed roots: θ_j ∈ (α_{j+1}, α_j), j = 1..N−1, the roots of Σ_p α_p z_p/(α_p − θ) = 1 − q.
Peaks (reboiler-referenced): H_j = Σ_{p>j} α_p z_p/(θ_j − α_p); V_Petlyuk := max_j H_j.

## 3. The class 𝒜 (finite arrangements)

An **arrangement** is a finite directed multigraph whose vertices are *units* and whose arcs are *streams*, plus a
feed source and product outlets. Each stream e has a source and a destination, a phase (saturated liquid or
saturated vapor), nonnegative component flows n_{e,i}, and vapor flow v_e (= Σ_i n_{e,i} for a vapor, 0 for a
liquid). Unit types:

- **(E) equilibrium stage** (a tray, a flash, a feed stage, a partial condenser or partial reboiler, a stage with
  an intermediate heat exchanger): any inflows; an external heat duty Q_u ∈ ℝ; its contents leave as a saturated
  liquid of composition x_u and a saturated vapor of composition y_u = K(x_u) x_u (componentwise), each divided into
  any number of outflow streams (a phase may have zero flow).
- **(M) single-phase mixer/splitter**: inflows of one phase; outflows of the same phase, all of the mixed
  composition; Q_u = 0.
- **(C) total condenser**: vapor inflows; liquid outflows of the mixed composition; Q_u = −(total vapor in).
- **(R) total vaporizer**: liquid inflows; vapor outflows of the mixed composition; Q_u = +(total liquid in).
- **Feed source**: the process feed, possibly divided into portions with its composition and thermal state, each
  sent to a unit. (Feed conditioning, a heater or cooler on a feed portion, is an (E) unit with duty receiving
  that portion; if 0 ≤ q ≤ 1 the feed may be separated into its two equilibrium phases by an (E) unit with Q = 0.)
- **Product outlets**: every stream leaving the arrangement is a product stream, assigned to one component p, of
  either phase, withdrawn from any unit. Several streams may carry the same product.

**Steady state.** Nonnegative flows such that at every unit u: (i) component balances
Σ_{e out of u} n_{e,i} = Σ_{e into u} n_{e,i} (the feed portion counted as an inflow); (ii) the CMO energy balance
Σ_{out} v_e − Σ_{in} v_e = Q_u (a feed portion of size f contributes vapor (1 − q) f as an inflow); (iii) the
composition rules of the unit type. Recycles (cycles of the graph) are allowed; the theory below needs no
assumption on stability or uniqueness of steady states.

**Objective.** R = Σ_u max(Q_u, 0), the total heat supplied = the total vaporization. Since Σ_u Q_u = (vapor in the
products) − (1 − q), R also equals the total cooling plus that constant.

**Adiabatic sections and columns** are special cases: an adiabatic section is a chain of (E) units with Q = 0,
liquid flowing down and vapor flowing up with constant flows L, V (CMO), and no other streams; a column is a stack
of sections joined at junction stages (feed stages, side draws, couplings). The definition of 𝒜 does not need
these notions; they matter only for the infinite-stage Underwood model (Section 6).

**HS-type devices are all in 𝒜**: conventional columns with total or partial condensers and reboilers; thermal
(direct) couplings (two-way vapor/liquid streams between stages of two columns, HS3 §6.1); side draws of either
phase; side strippers and rectifiers; Petlyuk, generalized Petlyuk and Kaibel arrangements, dividing-wall columns
(liquid split = several liquid outflows of a stage; vapor split = several vapor outflows; merging = several
inflows into a stage); several feed stages per column, intermediate sections between two feeds or side streams;
stream splitters dividing a stream among several columns, and remixing of any streams (fed to one stage or mixed in
an (M) unit); recycles; vapor or liquid bypasses (a stream from any unit to any unit; tray entrainment and weeping
are such streams: liquid of a stage's composition carried to the stage above or below); reverse net flows (no sign
condition on net flows); degenerate component distributions; pure products of either phase; feed splitting,
preheating, precooling, partial vaporization; heaters, coolers, intermediate reboilers and condensers at any stage
with external utilities (i.e. non-adiabatic sections, which HS's literal wording excludes: 𝒜 is larger than HS's
class); trays with a Murphree vapor efficiency E ∈ [0, 1] common to all components (exactly an (E) unit fed with
the fraction E of the vapor from below, plus an (M) bypass of the fraction 1 − E and an (M) mixer:
y_n = E K(x_n) x_n + (1 − E) y_{n−1}, same liquid, same balances; Murphree liquid efficiency: liquid bypass), hence
also packed sections as limits of such trays.

## 4. Infinite stages and pure products: the limit definition

A steady state s has **purity ≥ 1 − ε** if every product stream assigned to p has mole fraction of p at least
1 − ε. For any family 𝒮 ⊆ 𝒜 (for example all stage counts of one configuration, or all of 𝒜) put

  R*(𝒮; ε) = inf { R(s) : s a steady state of a member of 𝒮 with purity ≥ 1 − ε },
  R*(𝒮) = lim_{ε→0+} R*(𝒮; ε)   (nondecreasing as ε decreases, so the limit exists in [0, +∞]).

HS's "minimum vapor with infinitely many stages" of a configuration X is R*(𝒮_X), 𝒮_X = all arrangements with the
topology of X and any numbers of stages in its sections. This is the faithful reading: every real arrangement has
finitely many stages and impure products, and Underwood's infinite-stage values are the limits of these infima.
**The formal conjecture (HS3 §6.3):** R*(𝒮) ≥ R*(𝒮_Petlyuk) for every family 𝒮 ⊆ 𝒜, with
R*(𝒮_Petlyuk) = max_j H_j (HS's formula).

## 5. The stage-level bound (statement and complete proof)

Fix θ > 0 with θ ∉ {α_1, …, α_N}. The **Underwood flux** of a stream e is φ_e(θ) = v_e − Σ_i α_i n_{e,i}/(α_i − θ);
that of a feed portion of size f is f φ_F(θ) with φ_F(θ) = (1 − q) − Σ_i α_i z_i/(α_i − θ). By definition of the feed
roots, **φ_F(θ_j) = 0**. Write G_x(θ) = Σ_i α_i x_i/(α_i − θ). (For an adiabatic section with vapor V and upward net
flows w, φ(vapor up) − φ(liquid down) = V − Σ_i α_i w_i/(α_i − θ) at every cross-section: the section flux Λ_s of
result 12.)

**Lemma 5.1 (node balance).** At every unit u of a steady state, with f_u the feed portion entering u and product
streams counted as outflows: Σ_{e out of u} φ_e(θ) − Σ_{e into u} φ_e(θ) = Q_u + f_u φ_F(θ).
*Proof.* φ_e = v_e − Σ_i c_i n_{e,i} with c_i = α_i/(α_i − θ) is linear in (v_e, n_e). Subtract Σ_i c_i × (component
balance i) from the energy balance (ii) of §3. ∎

**Lemma 5.2 (sign uniformity).** A liquid stream of flow l and composition x has φ = −l G_x(θ). A vapor stream of
flow v and composition y has φ = v − v Σ_i α_i y_i/(α_i − θ) = −θ v Σ_i y_i/(α_i − θ) (since α_i/(α_i − θ) =
1 + θ/(α_i − θ) and Σ_i y_i = 1); if y is in equilibrium with x (y_i = α_i x_i/S(x)), then
Σ_i y_i/(α_i − θ) = G_x(θ)/S(x), so φ = −(θ v/S(x)) G_x(θ). Since θ > 0 and S(x) > 0, **all outflows of an (E) unit
have fluxes of the sign of −G_{x_u}(θ)**, and all outflows of an (M), (C) or (R) unit (one phase, one composition)
have fluxes of one sign. ∎

Fix j and θ = θ_j. Let 𝒰₊ be the set of units all of whose outflows have φ ≥ 0 ("heavy-ish") and 𝒰₋ the others
("light-ish"); by Lemma 5.2 the outflows of a unit of 𝒰₋ all have φ < 0 or zero flow. Put R(𝒲) = Σ_{u∈𝒲} max(Q_u, 0)
and Q(𝒲) = Σ_{u∈𝒲} max(−Q_u, 0).

**Theorem 5.3 (stage-level cut).** At every steady state of every arrangement of 𝒜 and for every j,

  R − Σ_P max(0, φ_P(θ_j)) = R(𝒰₋) + Q(𝒰₊) + Σ_{e: 𝒰₊→𝒰₋} φ_e(θ_j) − Σ_{e: 𝒰₋→𝒰₊} φ_e(θ_j),

the sums running over the streams between the two sets (P over the product streams), and every term on the right
is ≥ 0. In particular **R ≥ Σ_P max(0, φ_P(θ_j))**.
*Proof.* Sum Lemma 5.1 over u ∈ 𝒰₊ at θ_j (the feed terms vanish). Streams with both ends in 𝒰₊ cancel, so
R(𝒰₊) − Q(𝒰₊) = Σ_{P from 𝒰₊} φ_P + Σ_{𝒰₊→𝒰₋} φ_e − Σ_{𝒰₋→𝒰₊} φ_e. Products leaving 𝒰₊ have φ_P ≥ 0 and products
leaving 𝒰₋ have φ_P ≤ 0, so Σ_{P from 𝒰₊} φ_P = Σ_P max(0, φ_P). Streams leaving 𝒰₊ have φ ≥ 0 and streams leaving
𝒰₋ have φ ≤ 0, so both crossing sums contribute ≥ 0. Add R(𝒰₋) + Q(𝒰₊) to both sides (R = R(𝒰₊) + R(𝒰₋)). ∎

**Corollary 5.4 (pure products).** If every product stream is pure, R ≥ H_j for every j, i.e. R ≥ max_j H_j.
*Proof.* A pure liquid product of component p with flow P_p has φ = α_p P_p/(θ_j − α_p), a pure vapor one
φ = θ_j P_p/(θ_j − α_p); both are > 0 iff p > j, the vapor value being the larger; the products of p carry z_p. ∎

**Corollary 5.5 (finite purity).** If every product stream has purity ≥ 1 − ε, then for every j
R ≥ H_j − c_j ε, with c_j = max(α_j/(α_j − θ_j), α_{j+1}/(θ_j − α_{j+1})). Hence **R*(𝒮) ≥ max_j H_j for every
family 𝒮 ⊆ 𝒜**.
*Proof.* For equal component flows, φ(vapor) = φ(liquid) + total flow, so it suffices to bound liquid fluxes. Keeping
only the products assigned to components p > j: Σ_P max(0, φ_P) ≥ Σ_{i>j} α_i n^h_i/(θ_j − α_i) − Σ_{i≤j} α_i
n^h_i/(α_i − θ_j), n^h_i the flow of i in these products. By the overall component balance n^h_i = z_i − m_i for i > j,
m_i the flow of i in products assigned to components ≤ j. The m_i (i > j) and the n^h_i (i ≤ j) are disjoint parts of
the total impurity, which is ≤ ε × (total product flow) = ε; the coefficients α_i/(θ_j − α_i) (i > j, increasing in
α_i) and α_i/(α_i − θ_j) (i ≤ j, decreasing in α_i) are ≤ c_j. ∎

*What the proof uses, and nothing else:* the CMO energy balance at every unit; phase equilibrium with constant α at
(E) units, a single outflow composition and phase at (M), (C), (R) units; θ_j > 0 (α_N > 0); one feed composition
and thermal state (every feed portion has zero flux at θ_j); every positive duty counted in R. Nothing about
topology, numbers of stages, signs of net flows, distributions, stability, adiabaticity of sections, product phases.
Longer write-ups with the section and junction lemmas, the decomposition of physical devices into units and the tie
structure: `total/sections/sections.md`, `total/network/network.md`. Numerical checks (evidence only): four
independent solvers, `total/foundations/scripts/unitnet.py`, `total/sections/scripts/`, `total/network/scripts/`,
`total/simulation/scripts/`.

**Logical status of the conjecture.** Every arrangement satisfies R*(𝒮) ≥ max_j H_j, the Petlyuk arrangement
included. Hence **attainment**, R*(𝒮_Petlyuk) = max_j H_j (finite-stage Petlyuk steady states with purity → 1 and
R → max_j H_j; this is HS's formula for the Petlyuk minimum in the finite-stage-limit sense), **implies** the
conjecture "R*(𝒮_Petlyuk) ≤ R*(𝒮) for every 𝒮". (It is sufficient, not logically necessary: if attainment failed,
the conjecture could still hold, but HS's formula would be false.) Attainment is proved in the
infinite-stage Underwood model (result 12, Theorem achieve: every internal column at its preferred split). Its
finite-stage form is the subject of `total/simulation/` (numerics), `total/sections/` and `total/attainment-astra/`
(proof attempts).

**What a counterexample would have to violate** (red team): the CMO energy balance; sign uniformity, i.e. a unit
releasing streams of different compositions not in phase equilibrium and not decomposable into equilibrium stages,
bypasses and mixers (component-dependent efficiencies, membranes, decanters); the feed property φ_F(θ_j) = 0 (several
feeds of different compositions); constant α (non-ideal mixtures, several pressures); or the accounting of R (heat
delivered internally and not counted). All are outside HS's assumptions (Section 7).

## 6. The infinite-stage section model (context; not needed for Section 5)

For an adiabatic section with constant L, V > 0 and net upward component flows w_i = V y_i − L x_i (any signs),
Underwood's identity holds at every cross-section and for every θ (Th §2.5.2, eq. 2.49):
L G_{x_{n+1}}(θ) − (θ V / S(x_n)) G_{x_n}(θ) = Ψ(θ) := V − Σ_i α_i w_i/(α_i − θ). The section roots φ_k (Ψ(φ_k) = 0)
make G_{x_n}(φ_k) evolve geometrically from stage to stage, the pinch compositions are x_i ∝ w_i/(α_i − φ_k)
(Th §3.4.4, eq. 3.39), and an infinitely long section connects pinch zones (Th §3.4.3–3.4.6). Minimum vapor of a
conventional column ⇔ coincidence of top and bottom roots at the active common roots, between the volatilities of
the components distributing to both ends (Th §2.5.3, §3.2.3–3.2.4, §4.2.1). With reverse net flows the roots can
leave their intervals (Th §4.3.3–4.3.4).

**The infinite-stage section, as a limit of finite sections** (`total/sections/sections.md` §8.3, Lemma 11, with the
projective-linear form of `total/network/network.md`): at every cross-section x_above ∝ M(w) x_below with
M(w) = V diag(α) − w α^T, whose eigenvalues are V φ_r (φ_r the roots of Ψ) and V α_h for components h with zero net
flow, with left eigenvectors giving the Underwood functions G_x(φ_r) (resp. x_h). If finite-stage steady states with
n → ∞ stages converge (fixed L, V; net flows, bottom-end and top-end compositions converging) and the limit
eigenvalues have distinct moduli, there is a pinch index r* such that the Underwood functions of the bottom-end
composition vanish at every root above the pinch root, and those of the top-end composition at every root below it;
in particular **the liquid entering the top contains no component that the section removes** (Halvorsen's rule, Th
§4.3.1, obtained here as a theorem about limits). This is the faithful infinite-stage model of a section at every
operating point, with any signs of the net flows (distinct moduli permitting). The theorem of §5 does not need it.

The strict/permissive Underwood models of result 12 are formulations of
this infinite-stage behaviour column by column; the verifier showed they differ from physics at degenerate points
(strict too restrictive) and with excess vapor at active roots (permissive too permissive). The finite-stage model
of Sections 3–4 has neither problem.

## 7. Excluded, and why it is outside HS's statement

- **Internal heat exchange** between process streams, if the heat delivered internally is not counted in R: HS
  exclude it explicitly (HS3 abstract, §6.3, §8); Th §6.5 shows it lowers the external heat. If the internally
  delivered heat is counted (R = total vaporization), it is allowed, and the bound of Section 5 still holds.
- **Several pressures**: HS assume constant pressure; multi-pressure arrangements save energy only through heat
  exchange between columns (Th §6.6) or compression, and change α.
- **Non-ideal mixtures, variable α, non-CMO enthalpies**: outside HS's assumptions.
- **Non-equilibrium separation devices** (membranes, decanters, component-dependent efficiencies, rate-based
  Maxwell–Stefan models): not distillation in HS's equilibrium-stage, infinite-stage model. (Murphree trays with
  a common efficiency are in 𝒜, §3.)
- **Heat pumps and work input**: these move heat between temperature levels (internal heat exchange with work).
- **Several process feeds** of different compositions or thermal states: HS consider one feed (a feed that is
  split and conditioned is allowed).
- **Finite stages and impure products**: not excluded but handled by the limit of Section 4.
