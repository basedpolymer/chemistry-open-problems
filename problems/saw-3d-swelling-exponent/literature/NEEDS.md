# Literature agent: sources still needed (paywalled or unavailable)

Already supplied and read (thanks): Kesten 1963, 1964; Madras 1988; Madras 2014 (publisher); Chayes-Chayes 1986;
Hammersley-Welsh 1962; Madras-Slade excerpts (pp. 5-19, 30-37, 57-117, 171-174, 186-188, 198-205, 227-228).

Still useful, in order of value for the solvers:

1. N. Madras, Bounds on the critical exponent of self-avoiding polygons, in: Random Walks, Brownian Motion, and
   Interacting Particle Systems, Progr. Probab. 28, Birkhauser 1991, pp. 359-371, doi:10.1007/978-1-4612-0459-6_20
   (Crossref-verified). Reason: per Hammond (AoP 2018) it gives a generating-function bound on polygons which in d = 3
   "would assert lim theta_n >= 1"; the only d = 3-specific polygon-exponent statement I know of.
2. N. Madras, A rigorous bound on the critical exponent for the number of lattice trees, animals, and polygons,
   J. Stat. Phys. 78 (1995) 681-699, doi:10.1007/BF02183684 (Crossref-verified). Reason: polygon joining technique
   (d = 2) used by DGHM 2020 and Hammond; check whether any part is dimension-free.
3. Madras-Slade, The Self-Avoiding Walk, Chapter 8 "Polygons, slabs, bridges and knots" (pp. 257-279,
   doi:10.1007/978-1-4614-6025-1_8; Section 8.3 on infinite bridges per DCH) and
   Chapter 7 (Section 7.3-7.4, pattern theorem and ratio limits). Reason: possible statements on the critical
   bridge partition function B_T(z_c) and on the infinite bridge measure (needed for the KP route, lead 4).
4. Published versions of DCH 2013 (CMP) and KP 2026 (AoP) only if a solver needs to quote the published wording
   (the arXiv versions were read in full).

Not needed: Hara-Slade 1992 (d >= 5).
