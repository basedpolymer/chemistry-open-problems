\begin{paperabstract}
For conservative mass-action networks with a positive steady state and no two species with the same rate, Iosif
(arXiv:2603.08767, Conjecture~22 and Question~24) conjectured a ``duality relation'' between preclusters, which are
sets of reactions, and maximal invariant polyhedral supports (MIPS), which are sets of species. Since species and
rate constants exchange roles in his dual network, he also conjectured that preclusters and siphons are dual objects.
The relation is not defined in the paper, so we make each ingredient precise. Definition~17 and Remark~18 of the
paper agree: MIPS are the classes of species whose columns in a matrix of conservation laws are positively
proportional. The precluster construction of the paper and the iterated one of the author's thesis give the same
partition. Both hypotheses, conservativity and consistency, are exchanged by the duality. We then prove exact
criteria. The preclusters of a consistent network refine the MIPS of its dual if and only if any two reactions with a
common source are fully coupled. The two partitions coincide if and only if, in addition, every nontrivial class of
fully coupled reactions contains such a pair. The dual statements hold for species. Consequently every formulation
we could extract fails: equality, refinement in either direction and equal cardinality, on either side of the
duality and under three readings of MIPS. The counterexamples are tiny: \(X_1\rightleftarrows X_2\), the 3-cycle
\(X_1\to X_2\to X_3\to X_1\) (weakly reversible, deficiency zero, self-dual), the one-site futile cycle, and two
examples from the author's thesis. The siphon formulations of Question~24 and the claim of Remark~23 (internal cycles
refine preclusters) fail on the same networks. Exact computations on 15\,513 small networks confirm the criteria.
\end{paperabstract}

\section*{The question}

Iosif~\autocite{Iosif2026} develops a duality for mass-action networks. The dual of a network exchanges the roles of
species and rate constants: the dual has one species per reaction and one reaction per species. He proves that
conserved quantities are dual to internal cycles (his Theorem~21) and then states, in §3 (p.~7 of arXiv
v2, 15 July 2026; identical in v1 and in HAL v3):

\begin{openquote}
``Conjecture 22. For conservative mass-action networks that do not have two species with exactly the same rates and
have at least one positive steady state, there is a duality relation between the sets of preclusters and of maximal
invariant polyhedral supports.

Remark 23. The condition \(\dot x_i\neq\dot x_j\) for \(i\neq j\) is necessary in order to make sure no further
clustering on the left kernel is done. We require that there is at least one positive steady state so that the
internal cycles are a refinement of preclusters.

We conclude with the following question:

Question 24. Is there a duality relation between preclusters and siphons?''
\end{openquote}

The abstract and the introduction (p.~2) read: ``we conjecture that there is a duality relation between preclusters
and maximal invariant polyhedral supports. Given the close relation between maximal invariant polyhedral supports and
siphons, we also conjecture that siphons and preclusters are dual objects.'' The status of the statement has changed
over time. The 2020 HAL version~\autocite{Iosif2020} states it as a theorem, without the positive-steady-state
hypothesis and without proof. A 2024 talk~\autocite{Iosif2024talk} lists ``MIPS \(\dots\) are dual to preclusters'' as
a theorem of Ananiadi and Iosif. A 2025 poster abstract~\autocite{IosifAnaniadi2025} says ``we prove that preclusters
are dual to maximal invariant polyhedral supports''. The 2026 preprint calls it a conjecture. None of these texts
defines ``duality relation''. Preclusters come from the author's thesis~\autocite{Iosif2019}, Chapter~4, after the
clusters of Conradi and Flockerzi~\autocite{ConradiFlockerzi2012}.

\paragraph{What we prove.} In §1 we fix the definitions and show that the ambiguities in them are harmless
(\zcref{prop:mips,lem:variants}). In §2 we prove exact criteria for each comparison between preclusters and MIPS
(\zcref{thm:criteria,cor:species}). In §3 we use them to refute every formulation listed in \zcref{tab:formulations}
(\zcref{thm:main}). §4 reports an exhaustive exact check on small networks and §5 lists what remains open. Throughout,
\(A\refi B\) means that the partition (or family) \(A\) refines \(B\): every block of \(A\) is contained in a block of
\(B\).

\begin{theorem}[main result]\label{thm:main}
Read ``duality relation'' as any of the relations of \zcref{tab:formulations}: equality, refinement in either
direction, or equal number of blocks, between the preclusters of \(\Net^*\) and the MIPS of \(\Net\) (species side) or
between the preclusters of \(\Net\) and the MIPS of \(\Net^*\) (reaction side). Read MIPS in any of the three ways of
\zcref{rem:readings}. Then Conjecture~22 is false under every such reading. Question~24 has a negative answer for each
of the siphon relations of \zcref{tab:formulations}, and the claim of Remark~23 is false. Every counterexample
satisfies all hypotheses of Conjecture~22, has positive steady states, and has at most six species.
\end{theorem}

\section{Definitions, and why their ambiguities do not matter}

\paragraph{Networks.} A network \(\Net\) has species \(X_1,\dots,X_n\) and reactions \(R_j\colon y_j\to y'_j\)
(\(j=1,\dots,r\)) with \(y_j,y'_j\in\Z^n_{\ge0}\). The educt and product matrices are \(\Ye=(y_1|\cdots|y_r)\) and
\(\Yp=(y'_1|\cdots|y'_r)\), and \(\Gamma=\Yp-\Ye\) is the stoichiometric matrix. The mass-action system is
\(\dot x=\Gamma\operatorname{diag}(k)\,x^{\Ye}\) with \(k\in\R^r_{>0}\). The \emph{flux cone} is
\(C(\Net)=\ker\Gamma\cap\R^r_{\ge0}\). The \emph{conservation cone} is
\(K(\Net)=\{z\in\R^n_{\ge0}:z^{\mathsf T}\Gamma=0\}\). Both cones are pointed. The supports of the extreme rays of
\(C(\Net)\) are the internal cycles of \(\Net\) (Definition~20 of~\autocite{Iosif2026};
\autocite{SchusterHilgetag1994}). Let \(E\) be the \(r\times m\) matrix whose columns are the extreme rays of
\(C(\Net)\), and let \(n_1,\dots,n_r\in\R^m\) be its rows. Let \(W\) be the \(p\times n\) matrix whose rows are the
extreme rays of \(K(\Net)\), and let \(w_1,\dots,w_n\in\R^p\) be its columns. Positive rescaling of the rays does not
affect anything below.

\paragraph{Hypotheses of Conjecture~22.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(H1)] \emph{Conservative}: some \(z\in K(\Net)\) has all coordinates positive.
\item[(H2)] \emph{At least one positive steady state.} The paper treats the rate constants as parameters
(Definition~4 and Remark~5), so (H2) means that some \(k\) admits a positive steady state. This is equivalent to
consistency: \(C(\Net)\) contains a vector with all coordinates positive. Indeed, if \(x>0\) is a steady state for
\(k\), then \(v=\operatorname{diag}(k)x^{\Ye}>0\) lies in \(\ker\Gamma\). Conversely, if \(v>0\) lies in
\(\ker\Gamma\), then \(x=(1,\dots,1)\) is a steady state for \(k=v\).
\item[(H3)] \emph{No two species with exactly the same rates}: \(\dot x_i\neq\dot x_j\) for \(i\neq j\), as in
Remark~23. As polynomials in \((k,x)\), \(\dot x_i=\sum_j\Gamma_{ij}k_jx^{y_j}\), so (H3) says that the rows of
\(\Gamma\) are pairwise distinct.
\end{itemize}
Every formulation of \zcref{tab:formulations} is refuted by one of the networks \(T\), \(W\), \(U\), \(D\) below. These
satisfy the strongest reading of (H3): no row of \(\Gamma\) is a multiple of another, so \(\dot x_i\) and \(\dot x_j\)
are not even proportional. Every counterexample has a positive steady state for explicit rate constants, and
\(T\), \(W\), \(R\) and \(N_{4.12}\) have one for all rate constants.

\paragraph{The dual network.} Following §3 of~\autocite{Iosif2026} (see also~\autocite{BlanchiniGiordano2022}), the
dual \(\Net^*\) has species \(K_1,\dots,K_r\), one for each reaction of \(\Net\), and one reaction
\(\rho_i\colon K^{(\Ye)_{i\cdot}}\to K^{(\Yp)_{i\cdot}}\) for each species \(X_i\) of \(\Net\). Here
\((\Ye)_{i\cdot}\) denotes the \(i\)th row. The dual may contain the zero complex, and it contains a loop
\(\rho_i\colon y\to y\) if \(X_i\) is a pure catalyst. All definitions and proofs below apply verbatim to such
networks.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:dual}
\(\Ye(\Net^*)=\Ye^{\mathsf T}\), \(\Yp(\Net^*)=\Yp^{\mathsf T}\), \(\Gamma(\Net^*)=\Gamma^{\mathsf T}\) and
\(\Net^{**}=\Net\). Hence \(C(\Net^*)=K(\Net)\) and \(K(\Net^*)=C(\Net)\). In particular \(\Net\) satisfies (H1) if
and only if \(\Net^*\) satisfies (H2), and vice versa. \(\Net^*\) satisfies (H3) if and only if the columns of
\(\Gamma\) are pairwise distinct. The internal cycles of \(\Net^*\) are the supports of the extreme conservation laws
of \(\Net\), which is the content of Theorem~21 of~\autocite{Iosif2026}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All statements follow from the definition by transposition.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Maximal invariant polyhedral supports.} Assume (H1). Let \(Z\in\R^{q\times n}\) be a matrix whose rows
form a basis of the conservation space \(L=\{z:z^{\mathsf T}\Gamma=0\}\). For \(c\) in the cone \(Q=Z(\R^n_{\ge0})\),
the invariant polyhedron is \(P_c=\{x\in\R^n_{\ge0}:Zx=c\}\). By (H1) it is a nonempty polytope. Each vertex \(v\) of
\(P_c\) is decorated by its support \(\supp v\) (the monomial \(\prod_{i\in\supp v}x_i\) in~\autocite{Iosif2026}).
Distinct vertices have distinct supports: \(\{Ze_t:t\in\supp v\}\) is linearly independent, so \(v\) is the only
point of \(P_c\) with support \(\supp v\). The
\emph{decorated type} of \(P_c\) is its face lattice with these labels. The paper's Definition~17 reads:

\begin{openquote}
``A set of species \(\Sigma=\{x_i\mid i\in I\subseteq[n]\}\) is called an invariant polyhedral support if all
combinatorial types of decorated abstract invariant polyhedra are invariant under the map \(x_i\mapsto x_j\) for all
\(x_i,x_j\in\Sigma\). An invariant polyhedral support is maximal if it is maximal with respect to inclusion.''
\end{openquote}

Remark~18 adds that ``one needs to compute the chamber decomposition in the space of linear conserved quantities, and
decorate each ray with the corresponding variables. The set of all variables decorating an individual ray is a
maximal invariant polyhedral support.'' There are finitely many decorated types (the source cites~\autocite{DeLoeraKimOnnSantos2009}
for this; it is not used below). We read ``invariant'' as: for every \(c\in Q\), relabelling the vertices of
\(P_c\) by the transposition \(\tau=(i\,j)\) gives the same decorated type. The next proposition shows that this
reading agrees with Remark~18.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mips}
Assume (H1) and let \(i\neq j\). The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every \(c\in Q\), the decorated type of \(P_c\) is invariant under the transposition of \(x_i\) and \(x_j\).
\item \(Ze_i=\mu Ze_j\) for some \(\mu>0\): \(x_i\) and \(x_j\) decorate the same ray of the chamber decomposition
(Remark~18).
\item \(e_i-\mu e_j\in\operatorname{im}\Gamma\) for some \(\mu>0\).
\item \(w_i=\mu w_j\) for some \(\mu>0\).
\item \(z_i=\mu z_j\) for some \(\mu>0\) and every conservation law \(z\in L\).
\end{enumerate}
Write \(i\sim j\) when these hold. Then \(\sim\) is an equivalence relation. A set of species is an invariant polyhedral
support if and only if it lies in one class, and the MIPS of \(\Net\) are exactly the classes. In particular the MIPS
partition the species. We denote this partition by \(\Mips(\Net)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(2)\(\Rightarrow\)(1): Let \(\varphi\colon\R^n\to\R^n\) swap the coordinates \(i\) and \(j\) with weights:
\(\varphi(x)_i=x_j/\mu\), \(\varphi(x)_j=\mu x_i\), and \(\varphi(x)_t=x_t\) otherwise. Then
\(Z\varphi(x)=Zx\), because \(\mu x_iZe_j+(x_j/\mu)Ze_i=x_iZe_i+x_jZe_j\). Moreover \(\varphi\) preserves
\(\R^n_{\ge0}\), and \(\supp\varphi(x)=\tau(\supp x)\). So \(\varphi\) restricts to an affine automorphism of every
\(P_c\) that relabels supports by \(\tau\), and the decorated type is invariant.
(1)\(\Rightarrow\)(2): Take \(c=Ze_i\in Q\). The point \(e_i\) is a vertex of \(P_c\) with label \(\{i\}\). Invariance
gives a vertex with label \(\{j\}\), that is, a point \(ae_j\in P_c\) with \(a>0\). Hence \(Ze_i=aZe_j\). Note also
\(Ze_i\neq0\), since \(z_i>0\) for the positive conservation law.
(2)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(3): \(\ker Z=L^{\perp}=\operatorname{im}\Gamma\).
(2)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(5): the rows of \(Z\) span \(L\).
(4)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(5): by (H1), \(K(\Net)\) contains an interior point of \(L\), so it spans \(L\). Being pointed,
it is generated by its extreme rays, so the rows of \(W\) span \(L\).
The relation in (2) is clearly an equivalence. Definition~17 asks for invariance under every transposition of two
elements of \(\Sigma\), so the invariant polyhedral supports are the subsets of classes, and the maximal ones are the
classes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[three readings of MIPS]\label{rem:readings}
\zcref{prop:mips} is the reading that matches Remark~18 (``ray reading''). Two other readings are conceivable.
The \emph{set reading} only asks that the set of all decorated types be invariant under \(\tau\). Its classes are
unions of ray classes and can be strictly larger: for \(Y\colon X_1+2X_2\rightleftarrows X_3\) the ray reading gives
\(\{X_1\},\{X_2\},\{X_3\}\) and the set reading gives \(\{X_1,X_2\},\{X_3\}\). The set reading contradicts Remark~18.
The \emph{semiflow reading} takes the supports of the extreme conservation laws, that is, the internal cycles of
\(\Net^*\). It is suggested by the diagram at the end of §3 of~\autocite{Iosif2026}, which links internal cycles and
MIPS by an arrow labelled ``dual''. For \(Y\) it gives \(\{X_1,X_3\},\{X_2,X_3\}\), which is not a partition. All
three readings agree in Example~25 of~\autocite{Iosif2026}, so that example cannot distinguish them. Our
counterexamples work for all three readings (\zcref{tab:formulations}). Our scripts compute the set reading whenever
\(q\le2\).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Siphons.} A \emph{siphon} is a nonempty set \(S\) of species such that every reaction with a product
species in \(S\) has a reactant species in \(S\) (Definition~13 of~\autocite{Iosif2026};
\autocite{ShiuSturmfels2010,AngeliDeLeenheerSontag2007}). A siphon is minimal if no proper subset is a siphon
(Definition~14). We write \(\Sip(\Net)\) for the family of minimal siphons. The support of a semi-positive
conservation law is a siphon~\autocite{AngeliDeLeenheerSontag2007}. This is presumably the ``close relation'' between
MIPS and siphons mentioned in the source, at least in the semiflow reading of \zcref{rem:readings}.

\paragraph{Preclusters.} Two distinct reactions with the same source form a \emph{doubling pair} (thesis
terminology~\autocite{Iosif2019}). Let \(G\) be the graph on \([r]\) whose edges are the doubling pairs. The paper's
Definition~19 reads:

\begin{openquote}
``Consider the graph \(\mathfrak G\) over \([r]\) which has an edge \(\{i,j\}\) whenever the \(i\)th and the \(j\)th
arrows have the same source and let \(n_1,\dots,n_r\) denote the rows of a matrix with columns the rays of the
stoichiometric cone. If to the graph \(\mathfrak G\) we adjoin the edges \(\{i,s\}\) and \(\{j,s\}\) whenever \(n_s\)
is in the span of \(\{n_i,n_j\}\) for each edge \(\{i,j\}\) of \(\mathfrak G\), we obtain the preclustering graph. A
precluster is a connected component of the preclustering graph.''
\end{openquote}

Call this graph \(G^+\). The thesis (Definition~4.3 and Algorithm~4.4 of~\autocite{Iosif2019}) iterates instead.
Starting from \(P=G\), while some edge \(\{i,j\}\) of \(P\) and some \(s\) outside the component of \(i\) satisfy
\(n_s\in\spn\{n_i,n_j\}\), it adjoins \(\{i,s\}\) and \(\{j,s\}\). Let \(\overline G\) be the smallest graph that
contains \(G\) and is closed under this adjunction rule without the component restriction.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:variants}
\(G^+\), \(\overline G\) and every output of Algorithm~4.4 (in any order of choices) have the same connected
components. We write \(\Pre(\Net)\) for this partition of the reactions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a doubling pair \(e=\{i,j\}\) put \(V_e=\spn\{n_i,n_j\}\). We claim that every edge \(\{a,b\}\) of
\(\overline G\) has \(\spn\{n_a,n_b\}\subseteq V_e\) for some doubling pair \(e\) with \(a,b\) in the
\(G^+\)-component of \(e\). This holds for the edges of \(G\). Suppose it holds for an edge \(\{a,b\}\) with pair
\(e=\{i,j\}\), and let \(n_s\in\spn\{n_a,n_b\}\subseteq V_e\). If \(s\notin e\), then \(G^+\) contains \(\{i,s\}\),
so in all cases \(s\) lies in the \(G^+\)-component of \(e\). Also \(\spn\{n_a,n_s\}\) and \(\spn\{n_b,n_s\}\) lie in \(V_e\). This proves the claim.
So every edge of \(\overline G\) lies inside a \(G^+\)-component. Since \(E(G^+)\subseteq E(\overline G)\), the two
graphs have the same components.

Let \(A\) be an output of Algorithm~4.4. Then \(E(A)\subseteq E(\overline G)\), so the components of \(A\) refine those
of \(\overline G\). Conversely, every edge of \(G^+\) lies inside a component of \(A\). For edges of \(G\) this holds
because \(G\subseteq A\). For an adjoined edge \(\{i,s\}\) with \(n_s\in V_{\{i,j\}}\), the algorithm cannot stop
while \(s\) lies outside the component of \(i\). Hence the components of \(G^+\) refine those of \(A\), and all three
partitions coincide.
\end{proof}

The enumeration of §4 confirms that the three constructions never differ. Under (H2) every reaction lies in an
internal cycle, so \(n_s\neq0\) for all \(s\). This is why the paper needs (H2): a reaction with \(n_s=0\) lies in every
span and would be glued to every doubling pair.

\paragraph{Full coupling.} Assume (H2). Reactions \(s,t\) are \emph{fully coupled} if \(n_s=\mu n_t\) for some
\(\mu>0\). Equivalently, \(v_s=\mu v_t\) for every \(v\in\ker\Gamma\), since the extreme rays span \(\ker\Gamma\) by
(H2). This is the full coupling of flux coupling analysis~\autocite{Burgard2004}, and the classes are the ``enzyme
subsets'' of~\autocite{Pfeiffer1999}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:dualmips}
If \(\Net\) satisfies (H2), then \(\Net^*\) satisfies (H1), and \(\Mips(\Net^*)\) is the partition of the reactions of
\(\Net\) into classes of fully coupled reactions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply \zcref{prop:mips}(4) to \(\Net^*\). By \zcref{lem:dual}, the extreme conservation laws of \(\Net^*\) are the
extreme rays of \(C(\Net)\), so the columns \(w^*_s\) are the rows \(n_s\).
\end{proof}

In the same way, by \zcref{prop:mips}(4), \(\Mips(\Net)\) groups species \(i,j\) with \(w_i=\mu w_j\). Call such
species \emph{conservation-coupled}.

\section{Exact criteria}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:criteria}
Let \(\Net\) be consistent (H2). Let \(\Pre=\Pre(\Net)\), and let \(\Mips\) be the partition into fully coupled classes,
which equals \(\Mips(\Net^*)\) by \zcref{prop:dualmips}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\Pre\refi\Mips\) if and only if every doubling pair is fully coupled.
\item \(\Mips\refi\Pre\) if and only if, for every fully coupled class \(\kappa\) with \(|\kappa|\ge2\), the common
line \(\R n_s\) (\(s\in\kappa\)) lies in \(\spn\{n_i,n_j\}\) for some doubling pair \(\{i,j\}\). In particular
\(\Mips\refi\Pre\) holds if every such class contains a reaction that shares its source with another reaction.
\item \(\Pre=\Mips\) if and only if every doubling pair is fully coupled and every fully coupled class with at least
two elements contains a doubling pair.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By (H2), \(n_s\neq0\) for all \(s\). Two nonzero nonnegative vectors that are linearly dependent are positive multiples
of each other, so \(n_s\in\R n_i\) means that \(s\) and \(i\) are fully coupled. We use \(G^+\) (\zcref{lem:variants}).

(1) If \(\Pre\refi\Mips\), the two reactions of a doubling pair lie in one precluster, hence in one coupled class.
Conversely, assume that all doubling pairs are fully coupled. For a doubling pair \(\{i,j\}\) we then have
\(\spn\{n_i,n_j\}=\R n_i\). So every \(s\) adjoined through \(\{i,j\}\) is fully coupled to \(i\). Thus every edge of
\(G^+\) joins fully coupled reactions, and \(\Pre\refi\Mips\).

(2) Suppose the condition holds for a class \(\kappa\) with the pair \(\{i,j\}\). Every \(t\in\kappa\) lies in
\(\{i,j\}\) or is joined to \(i\) by an edge of \(G^+\), so \(\kappa\) lies in one precluster. Conversely, suppose
\(\kappa\) lies in one precluster and \(|\kappa|\ge2\). Then any \(t\in\kappa\) is not isolated in \(G^+\). By the
definition of \(G^+\), either \(t\) belongs to a doubling pair \(\{t,j\}\), and then \(n_t\in\spn\{n_t,n_j\}\); or
\(t\) was adjoined through a doubling pair \(\{i,j\}\) with \(n_t\in\spn\{n_i,n_j\}\). In both cases the condition
holds.

(3) Assume both conditions. Then \(\Pre\refi\Mips\) by (1). If a class \(\kappa\) with \(|\kappa|\ge2\) contains the
doubling pair \(\{i,j\}\), then \(n_\kappa\in\spn\{n_i,n_j\}\), so (2) gives \(\Mips\refi\Pre\). Conversely, if
\(\Pre=\Mips\), then (1) gives the first condition. Take a class \(\kappa\) with \(|\kappa|\ge2\) and \(t\in\kappa\).
As in (2), \(t\) is in a doubling pair or adjoined through one, \(\{i,j\}\), with \(n_t\in\spn\{n_i,n_j\}=\R n_i\). So
\(t\) is fully coupled to \(i\) and to \(j\), and \(\{i,j\}\subseteq\kappa\).
\end{proof}

Two species are \emph{twins} if they have identical rows of \(\Ye\), that is, equal coefficients in every source
complex. The twin pairs of \(\Net\) are exactly the doubling pairs of \(\Net^*\).

\begin{corollary}[species side]\label{cor:species}
Let \(\Net\) be conservative (H1). Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\Pre(\Net^*)\refi\Mips(\Net)\) if and only if all twin pairs are conservation-coupled;
\item \(\Mips(\Net)\refi\Pre(\Net^*)\) if and only if, for every MIPS \(\Sigma\) with \(|\Sigma|\ge2\), the line
\(\R w_\Sigma\) lies in \(\spn\{w_i,w_j\}\) for some twin pair \(\{i,j\}\);
\item \(\Pre(\Net^*)=\Mips(\Net)\) if and only if all twin pairs are conservation-coupled and every MIPS with at least
two elements contains a twin pair.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \zcref{thm:criteria} to \(\Net^*\), which is consistent by \zcref{lem:dual}. Its doubling pairs are the twin
pairs. Its vectors \(n^*_i\) are the columns \(w_i\). Its fully coupled classes are \(\Mips(\Net)\), by
\zcref{prop:dualmips} applied to \(\Net^*\) together with \(\Net^{**}=\Net\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[product complexes are sources]\label{cor:wr}
Assume (H1) and (H3), and assume that every product complex of \(\Net\) is also a source complex. This holds, for
example, when \(\Net\) is weakly reversible. Then \(\Net\) has no twins, so \(\Pre(\Net^*)\) is the partition into
singletons. Hence \(\Pre(\Net^*)\refi\Mips(\Net)\) always holds. Moreover, \(\Pre(\Net^*)=\Mips(\Net)\) holds if and
only if \(\operatorname{im}\Gamma\) contains no nonzero vector supported on exactly two coordinates.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(X_i,X_j\) are twins. Every product complex is a source complex, so its \(i\)th and \(j\)th coordinates are
equal. Hence rows \(i\) and \(j\) of \(\Yp\) are also equal, and so are rows \(i\) and \(j\) of \(\Gamma\). This
contradicts (H3). The last claim follows from \zcref{prop:mips}(3). Under (H1), a nonzero vector of
\(\operatorname{im}\Gamma\) cannot be supported on a single coordinate, since \(z^{\mathsf T}e_i=z_i>0\) for the
positive conservation law \(z\). A vector supported on \(\{i,j\}\) has the form \(a(e_i-\mu e_j)\) with \(\mu>0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[weakly reversible networks, reaction side]\label{cor:wrB}
Let \(\Net\) be weakly reversible, with \(y_j\neq y'_j\) for all \(j\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item If some complex is the source of two reactions, then \(\Pre(\Net)\nrefi\Mips(\Net^*)\).
\item If moreover \(\Net\) has deficiency zero, then \(\Pre(\Net)\neq\Mips(\Net^*)\) in all cases.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a directed cycle \(\gamma\) in the reaction graph, the indicator vector \(1_\gamma\) lies in \(C(\Net)\), because
the reaction vectors \(y'-y\) along \(\gamma\) sum to zero. A weakly reversible network is consistent, since the sum
of such indicators over a cycle cover is positive. (1) Let \(R_a\colon y\to y_1\) and \(R_b\colon y\to y_2\). By weak
reversibility there is a shortest directed path from \(y_1\) to \(y\). It does not pass through \(y\) before its end,
so it does not use \(R_b\). Together with \(R_a\) it forms a cycle \(\gamma\) with \((1_\gamma)_a=1\) and
\((1_\gamma)_b=0\). So \(R_a,R_b\) are not fully coupled, and \zcref{thm:criteria}(1) applies. (2) If some complex
has two outgoing reactions, use (1). Otherwise every complex has exactly one outgoing reaction, since every complex of
a weakly reversible network is a source. Each linkage class is strongly connected, so it is a directed cycle of length
\(\ge2\). With deficiency zero, \(\ker\Gamma\) is the kernel of the incidence map~\autocite{Feinberg2019}. So every
\(v\in\ker\Gamma\) has equal inflow and outflow at each complex, and all reactions of one cycle are fully coupled.
This gives a class with at least two elements and no doubling pair, so \zcref{thm:criteria}(3) fails.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \zcref{thm:main}]
Each entry of \zcref{tab:formulations} is checked by hand below and by exact computation
(\texttt{certify\_examples.py}, 132 claims). For every network, \(k=\sum_{\text{extreme flux rays}}v\) and
\(x=(1,\dots,1)\) give a positive steady state, so (H2) holds. Conservativity and (H3) are checked directly.

\emph{\(T\).} \(\Gamma\) has rows \((-1,0,1),(1,-1,0),(0,1,-1)\). They are pairwise linearly independent, and so are
the columns. \(\ker\Gamma\) and the conservation space are both spanned by \((1,1,1)\). \(T\) is weakly reversible
with deficiency \(3-1-2=0\), so it is complex balanced and has positive steady states for every
\(k\)~\autocite{HornJackson1972,Feinberg2019}. The three sources are distinct, so \(G\) has no edge and \(\Pre(T)\)
consists of singletons. The flux cone is the ray \((1,1,1)\), so all \(n_s\) are equal and \(\Mips(T^*)\) is one
block. The dual is \(T^*\colon K_1\to K_3\to K_2\to K_1\), again a 3-cycle. So the species side is the same:
\(\Pre(T^*)\) consists of singletons, while \(\Mips(T)=\{X_1,X_2,X_3\}\). The latter holds in all three readings,
because \(q=1\) and the only extreme conservation law is \((1,1,1)\). A nonempty siphon containing \(X_i\) must
contain the source of the reaction producing \(X_i\), so \(\{X_1,X_2,X_3\}\) is the only siphon of \(T\), and likewise
of \(T^*\). This refutes A1, A3, A4, B1, B3, B4, C, Q1, Q2, Q4, Q5 and Q6. The internal cycle \(\{R_1,R_2,R_3\}\)
lies in no precluster, which refutes R23.

\emph{\(R\).} The same argument with two species: \(\Pre(R)=\{\{R_1\},\{R_2\}\}\), \(\Mips(R^*)=\{\{R_1,R_2\}\}\),
and \(R^*\cong R\). No network with fewer species or reactions satisfies (H1) and (H2): with one species, (H1)
forces \(\Gamma=0\), and with one reaction, (H2) forces its reaction vector to vanish.

\emph{\(W\) and \(W^*\).} The flux cone of \(W\) has extreme rays \((1,0,1,0)\) and \((0,1,0,1)\). So
\(n_1=n_3=(1,0)\) and \(n_2=n_4=(0,1)\). The doubling pair \(\{R_1,R_2\}\) is not fully coupled, so
\(\Pre(W)\nrefi\Mips(W^*)\) by \zcref{thm:criteria}(1). Explicitly, \(\Pre(W)\) is one block, while
\(\Mips(W^*)=\{\{R_1,R_3\},\{R_2,R_4\}\}\) in all three readings. \(W\) is weakly reversible with deficiency zero, and
its rows of \(\Gamma\) are pairwise linearly independent and its columns distinct. By \zcref{lem:dual}, \(W^*\) satisfies
(H1)--(H3). Its species side is the reaction side of \(W\), which refutes A2.

\emph{\(U\).} The flux cone is the ray \((1,1,1)\). The extreme conservation laws are \((1,1,0,1)\) and
\((2,0,1,1)\), so \(w_1=(1,2)\), \(w_2=(1,0)\), \(w_3=(0,1)\) and \(w_4=(1,1)\). These are pairwise non-proportional,
so \(\Mips(U)\) consists of singletons (the set reading agrees). \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) are twins (both occur only in the
source \(X_1+X_2\)) and are not conservation-coupled. So \(\Pre(U^*)\nrefi\Mips(U)\) by \zcref{cor:species}(1).
Indeed \(\spn\{w_1,w_2\}=\R^2\), so \(\Pre(U^*)\) is one block. In the semiflow reading the supports are
\(\{X_1,X_2,X_4\}\) and \(\{X_1,X_3,X_4\}\), and neither contains all four species. The rows and the columns of
\(\Gamma(U)\) are pairwise linearly independent, so \(U\) and \(U^*\) satisfy the strongest form of (H3). By
\zcref{cor:wr}, a counterexample to A2 cannot be weakly reversible.

\emph{\(D\).} The flux cone is the ray \((1,1,1)\) and the only conservation law is \(X_1+X_2+X_3\). So the doubling
pair \(\{R_1,R_2\}\) and the twins \(X_2,X_3\) are coupled, and \(\Pre(D)=\Mips(D^*)\) and \(\Pre(D^*)=\Mips(D)\) are
the one-block partitions (\zcref{thm:criteria}(3)). The two equalities of Conjecture~22 hold for \(D\). The dual
\(D^*\colon K_1+K_2\to2K_3,\ K_3\to K_1,\ K_3\to K_2\) is isomorphic to \(D\). The minimal siphons of \(D\) are
\(\{X_1,X_2\}\) and \(\{X_1,X_3\}\): \(\{X_1\}\) is not a siphon because \(X_1\) is produced from \(X_2+X_3\), and
\(\{X_2\}\), \(\{X_3\}\) are not siphons because they are produced from \(X_1\). The single precluster lies in neither,
which refutes Q3 on both sides. The rows and the columns of \(\Gamma(D)\) are pairwise linearly independent.

\emph{\(F\).} Order the species \(S_0,S_1,E,F,ES_0,FS_1\) and the reactions as written. The extreme flux rays are
\(R_1+R_2\), \(R_4+R_5\) and \(R_1+R_3+R_4+R_6\). The extreme conservation laws are \(E+ES_0\), \(F+FS_1\) and
\(S_0+S_1+ES_0+FS_1\). The doubling pairs are \(\{R_2,R_3\}\) and \(\{R_5,R_6\}\), and neither is fully coupled. The
only fully coupled pair is \(\{R_3,R_6\}\). So \(\Pre(F)\) is one block, while
\(\Mips(F^*)=\{\{R_3,R_6\},\{R_1\},\{R_2\},\{R_4\},\{R_5\}\}\). The twins are \((S_0,E)\) and \((S_1,F)\), and they are
not conservation-coupled. So \(\Pre(F^*)\) is one block, while \(\Mips(F)=\{\{S_0,S_1\},\{E\},\{F\},\{ES_0\},\{FS_1\}\}\).
In the semiflow reading the supports are those of the three conservation laws, and none contains all species.
Likewise, on the reaction side no internal cycle of \(F\) contains all reactions. The minimal siphons of \(F\) are \(\{E,ES_0\}\), \(\{F,FS_1\}\) and \(\{S_0,S_1,ES_0,FS_1\}\). Those of \(F^*\) are
\(\{R_1,R_2\}\), \(\{R_4,R_5\}\) and \(\{R_1,R_3,R_4,R_6\}\). In both cases the single precluster lies in no minimal
siphon, which refutes Q3.

\emph{\(N_3\) and \(N_{4.12}\).} For \(N_3\) the internal cycles are \(\{R_1,R_2\}\) and \(\{R_2,R_3\}\) (thesis
Example~1.22). So \(n_1=(1,0)\), \(n_2=(1,1)\), \(n_3=(0,1)\), and the doubling pair \(\{R_2,R_3\}\) is not fully
coupled. Hence \(\Pre(N_3)=\{\{R_1,R_2,R_3\}\}\) (thesis Example~4.5), while \(\Mips(N_3^*)\) consists of singletons
(also in the set reading; in the semiflow reading it consists of the two internal cycles, neither of which contains
all three reactions).
For \(N_{4.12}\) the thesis finds the preclusters \(\{R_1\},\{R_2\}\). The flux cone is the ray \((1,1)\), so
\(\Mips(N_{4.12}^*)=\{\{R_1,R_2\}\}\), and the internal cycle \(\{R_1,R_2\}\) lies in no precluster, although
\(N_{4.12}\) has positive steady states for all \(k\). Both networks satisfy (H1)--(H3).

The entry ``every WR deficiency-0 network'' for B1 is \zcref{cor:wrB}(2). The entries \(U\) for A1 and \(W\),
\(N_3\) for B1 follow from the failures of A2 and B2, since equality implies refinement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
In Example~25 of~\autocite{Iosif2026}, \(\Net^*\) has the single precluster \(\{x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\}\), while the
MIPS of \(\Net\) are \(\{x_1,x_3\}\) and \(\{x_2,x_4\}\). This already contradicts A1 and A2, but that network has no positive
steady state (\(\dot x_2=k_1x_3x_4\)), so it lies outside Conjecture~22. The counterexamples above lie inside. The
diagram at the end of §3 of~\autocite{Iosif2026} also links ``internal cycles'' and ``MIPS'' by an arrow labelled
``dual''. For \(F\), the internal cycles of \(F^*\) are the supports of the conservation laws above, which differ from
the ray-reading MIPS of \(F\).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[clusters]\label{rem:clusters}
Conradi and Flockerzi~\autocite{ConradiFlockerzi2012} (§3.2, Assumption~2, Fact~2, Algorithm~1) call
\(e_l-e_k\in\ker\Ye\) a doubling vector; these are our doubling pairs. Their Fact~2(a) is the span rule of
Definition~19: on the pairs \((\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda(E)^2\) with
\(n_l\lambda/n_l\mu=n_k\lambda/n_k\mu\), every \(j\) with \(n_j\in\spn\{n_l,n_k\}\) has the same ratio. Their
clusters group the rows with equal ratio on the pairs that satisfy these doubling relations, where
\(\Lambda(E)=\{\lambda\ge0:E\lambda>0\}\). This definition does not involve rate constants. The thesis
(Definition~4.6~\autocite{Iosif2019}) fixes \(k\) instead (its Remark~4.9). In both versions every precluster lies in a
cluster. For the two-site phosphorylation network of \zcref{tab:motifs} (their network \(N_1\), with the same
reaction order), their clusters \(\{1,2,3,10,11,12\}\) and \(\{4,\dots,9\}\) (their Example~1) coincide with our
preclusters. The 2024 talk~\autocite{Iosif2024talk} conjectures that siphons and clusters are dual. The cluster
version of B1 fails as well. For \(N_3\), the doubling relation \((\lambda_1+\lambda_2)/(\mu_1+\mu_2)=\lambda_2/\mu_2\)
with \(\lambda,\mu>0\) forces \(\mu\in\R_{>0}\lambda\), so all three ratios coincide and the Conradi--Flockerzi
cluster is \(\{R_1,R_2,R_3\}\). The thesis finds the same single cluster for \(k_2>k_3\) (Example~4.7). Yet no two
reactions of \(N_3\) are fully coupled.
\end{remark}

\section{What is settled and what remains open}

\paragraph{Settled.} The conjecture is false under every reading of ``duality relation'' as an equality, a refinement
in either direction, or an equality of cardinalities between the precluster partition and the MIPS partition, on
either side of the network duality and for every reading of MIPS. This includes the reading by analogy with
Theorem~21, \(\Pre(\Net^*)=\Mips(\Net)\) or \(\Pre(\Net)=\Mips(\Net^*)\), which we consider the most plausible.
\zcref{thm:criteria} and \zcref{cor:species} determine exactly the networks for which these relations hold. The six
siphon relations of Question~24 fail as well, and so does the claim of Remark~23.

\paragraph{Open.} (i) Conjecture~22 does not define ``duality relation''. A relation of a different kind (for
instance a Galois correspondence that is not a refinement) is not excluded by our counterexamples. Any relation that
forces a bijection between blocks, or a refinement, is excluded. (ii) We examined clusters (in the thesis and in
Conradi--Flockerzi) only on \(N_3\) and on the two-site phosphorylation network (\zcref{rem:clusters}). (iii) The paper motivates the conjecture by a possible new case of the Global
Attractor Conjecture in small codimension (§3 of~\autocite{Iosif2026}). We have no result on that.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Our reading of Definition~17 (\zcref{prop:mips}) quantifies over all \(c\in Q\), including
values on the boundary of chambers. This is the reading under which Remark~18 holds. The counterexamples were checked
under the other two readings as well. The paper defines preclusters only through Definition~19. We used the thesis
for the iterated version and for the examples \(N_3\) and \(N_{4.12}\). The original definitions of Conradi and
Flockerzi~\autocite{ConradiFlockerzi2012} (doubling vectors, the span rule, clusters) agree with those of the thesis
on the points used here (\zcref{rem:clusters}). No verification has been carried
out yet.
